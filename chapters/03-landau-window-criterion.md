## 3.1 Motivation

Every analytic number theorist knows the equivalence between zero-free half-planes for $L(s,\chi)$
and two-sided power-saving error terms in the prime number theorem. If we form the Riesz-smoothed
(or Cesàro-weighted) prime sum
$$S_\chi(y) = \sum_{1 \le n \le y} \Lambda(n)\,\chi(n)\Bigl(\frac{y}{n} - 1\Bigr)$$
and subtract its main term at $s = 1$ to obtain a normalized remainder $G_\chi(y)$ (defined
precisely in Section 3.3.1), then Mellin inversion — the explicit formula — expresses $G_\chi(y)$
roughly as a sum over the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $L(s,\chi)$:
$$G_\chi(y) = -\sum_{\rho} \frac{y^\rho}{\rho(\rho - 1)} + O(1) \qquad (y \ge 2)$$
[Expository] (compare H. Davenport, *Multiplicative Number Theory*, 3rd ed., Springer, 2000,
Chapter 17, or H. L. Montgomery and R. C. Vaughan, *Multiplicative Number Theory I: Classical
Theory*, Cambridge University Press, 2007, Chapters 12 and 15).

If one could prove a **two-sided** bound $\lvert G_\chi(y) \rvert \le C y^\theta$ for *all*
$y \ge 1$, the deduction that $L(s,\chi) \neq 0$ for $\Re s > \theta$ would take three lines: the
Mellin integral $\int_1^\infty G_\chi(y) y^{-s-1}\,dy$ would converge absolutely and define a
holomorphic function on $\Re s > \theta$, whereas by direct calculation that Mellin integral
equals $-\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi) / (s(s-1))$ up to terms holomorphic at $\Re s > 0$, forcing
$\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi)$ to have no poles on $\Re s > \theta$ [Expository].

The arithmetic engine of Part II (Chapters 5–11) falls short of that textbook ideal in **three
distinct ways**, and the architecture of Chapter 3 is designed specifically to overcome all three
at once [Expository]:

1. **Only a one-sided upper bound is available.** Chebyshev- or Stepanov-type positivity arguments
   produce inequalities with a single sign: in Chapter 5, `core_gm` bounds a prime-sum combination
   from *above*, $G_m(x) \le x^{1/2 + O(\varepsilon)}$, and says nothing directly against $G_m(x)$
   being large and negative [Lean] (`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/PrimitiveCosets/CoreGm.lean:12`).
   Why can a one-sided bound $G_m(y) \le C y^\theta$ rule out zeros on $\Re s > \theta$?
   - If $\rho = \beta \in (\theta, 1)$ is a **real** zero, notice the sign of its explicit-formula
     contribution: because $0 < \beta < 1$, we have $\beta(\beta - 1) < 0$, so
     $$-\frac{y^\beta}{\beta(\beta - 1)} = +\frac{y^\beta}{\beta(1 - \beta)} > 0!$$
     A real zero pushes $G_m(y)$ *upward*, straight into conflict with $G_m(y) \le C y^\theta$.
     (Had the prime sum been defined with the opposite sign, an upper bound could *never* rule out
     a Siegel zero $\beta$ near $1$!)
   - If $\rho = \beta + i\tau$ ($\tau \neq 0$, $\beta > \theta$) is a **complex** zero, then in a
     real-valued combination its conjugate $\bar\rho = \beta - i\tau$ also appears, contributing a
     real oscillatory wave $\approx 2 \Re\bigl(\frac{-y^{\beta + i\tau}}{\rho(\rho - 1)}\bigr) = A y^\beta \cos(\tau \log y + \phi_0)$.
     Because cosine changes sign every $\pi / \lvert \tau \rvert$ units of $\log y$, the wave cannot
     stay negative forever — as E. Landau ("Über einen Satz von Tschebyschef", *Mathematische
     Annalen* 61 (1905), 527–550, doi:10.1007/BF01449495) and G. Pólya ("Über das Vorzeichen des
     Restgliedes im Primzahlsatz", *Nachrichten von der Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu
     Göttingen, Mathematisch-Physikalische Klasse* 1930, 19–27) showed by applying Landau's
     singularity theorem for nonnegative Dirichlet integrals (Montgomery–Vaughan, Theorem 1.7 and
     §15.1).

2. **The bound holds only on finite logarithmic windows $[Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$.** Classical Landau
   theory considers the Laplace integral $\int_0^\infty (C e^{\theta u} - g(u)) e^{-su}\,du$,
   which requires $g(u) \le C e^{\theta u}$ for **all** $u \ge u_0$ so that the integrand has
   constant sign on $[u_0, \infty)$. In this proof, however, both the auxiliary prime pairs
   $(q, d)$ of Chapter 4 (`PairEx`) and the Tate–Shafarevich bound of Chapter 11 (`VWin`) hold only
   on intervals $y \in [Y, Y^\lambda]$ (with $\lambda = 3/2$, i.e. $\delta = 1/2$) for an
   unbounded sequence of scales $Y \to \infty$ [Lean] (`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/WinPairs.lean:5`).
   Outside $[Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$, the archive knows only the trivial bound $\lvert G_m(y) \rvert \le C_0 y^2$.
   How do we localize Landau's theorem to $[U, (1+\delta)U]$ (where $u = \log y$, $U = \log Y$)?
   Instead of letting $s \downarrow \beta$ where the Laplace integral diverges, we fix a **large
   real abscissa $\sigma > 2$** (chosen solely in terms of $\delta$) where the Laplace transform
   $\Phi(s) = \int_0^\infty g(u) e^{-su}\,du$ converges effortlessly, and inspect its normalized
   $k$-th derivatives:
   $$A_k(s) := \frac{(-1)^k \Phi^{(k)}(s)}{k!} = \int_0^\infty g(u)\,\frac{u^k e^{-su}}{k!}\,du.$$
   As a function of $u > 0$, the Poisson/Gamma weight $w_k(u) = \frac{u^k e^{-\sigma u}}{k!}$ is a
   bell-shaped positive kernel peaking at $u = k/\sigma$ with relative width $O(k^{-1/2})$. Taking
   $k \approx (1 + \delta/2)\sigma U$ places the peak squarely in the middle of the good window
   $[U, (1+\delta)U]$! Outside the window, exponential tilting suppresses the $e^{2u}$ tails down
   to $O(\sigma^{-k})$, while a pole $\rho$ at distance $d < \sigma - \theta$ from $\sigma + i\gamma$
   forces $\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert \asymp d^{-k} \gg (\sigma - \theta)^{-k}$.

3. **Only a positive combination over a cyclic level can be isolated.** In Chapter 5, finite
   Fourier analysis on the character group of an abelian host field $E \subset \mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$
   cannot isolate $\Re G_\psi(y)$ for a single primitive character $\psi$ with nonnegative spatial
   weights; what it *can* form with nonnegative weights is a combination
   $$G_m(y) = G_\zeta(y) + \sum_{\chi \in \Lambda \setminus \{1\}} w_\chi\,\Re G_\chi(y)$$
   over all primitive characters $\chi$ arising from the cyclic group $\langle \psi \rangle$, with
   strictly positive weights $w_\chi > 0$ [Lean, read]
   (`src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/ExplicitFormulaErrorOfHostConditions.lean:36–38,121`).
   Why don't zeros of different characters in $\Lambda$ cancel one another inside $G_m(y)$? Because
   every $w_\chi$ is strictly positive, and at any complex number $\rho$, the multiplicity
   $m_\chi(\rho) = \operatorname{ord}_{s=\rho} L(s,\chi)$ is a **nonnegative integer**! In the
   Laplace transform $\Phi(s)$ of $G_m(e^u)$, the residue at $\rho$ is
   $$-\frac{1}{\rho(\rho - 1)} \sum_{\chi \in \Lambda} \frac{w_\chi}{2}\bigl(m_\chi(\rho) + m_{\bar\chi}(\rho)\bigr),$$
   in which every term has the *exact same complex phase* $-1/(\rho(\rho-1))$ and nonnegative
   weight. Once at least one character in the family vanishes at $\rho$, the sum is strictly
   nonzero — and at a real zero $\rho = \beta \in (0,1)$, strictly positive real!

## 3.2 Small examples

Two elementary calculations bring the analytic mechanism of `poleData` and `landau_window` into
sharp focus before we introduce the general formalism [Expository].

### 3.2.1 Why Riesz smoothing $(y/n - 1)_+$ beats a sharp cutoff

Consider the single Riesz ramp $r_n(y) = \max(y/n - 1, 0)$ for an integer $n \ge 1$. For $\Re s > 1$,
its Mellin transform over $(1, \infty)$ vanishes on $(1, n]$ (since $y/n - 1 \le 0$ there) and on
$(n, \infty)$ is elementary:
$$\int_1^\infty \max\Bigl(\frac{y}{n} - 1, 0\Bigr) y^{-s-1}\,dy
= \int_n^\infty \Bigl(\frac{1}{n} y^{-s} - y^{-s-1}\Bigr)\,dy
= \frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{n^{1-s}}{s - 1} - \frac{n^{-s}}{s}
= \frac{n^{-s}}{s(s - 1)}.$$
Summing against $\Lambda(n)\chi(n)$ on $\Re s > 1$ (justified by Tonelli's theorem since
$\sum_{n \ge 1} \Lambda(n) n^{-\Re s} < \infty$) immediately gives
$$\int_1^\infty \Bigl(\sum_{1 \le n \le y} \Lambda(n)\chi(n)\Bigl(\frac{y}{n} - 1\Bigr)\Bigr) y^{-s-1}\,dy
= \frac{1}{s(s - 1)} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^s}
= -\frac{L'(s,\chi)/L(s,\chi)}{s(s - 1)}.$$

Compare this with Chebyshev's unsmoothed sum $\psi(y,\chi) = \sum_{n \le y} \Lambda(n)\chi(n)$,
whose Mellin transform is $-\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi) / s$, or with the standard integrated sum
$\psi_1(y,\chi) = \int_1^y \psi(t,\chi)\,dt = \sum_{n \le y} \Lambda(n)\chi(n)(y - n)$, which
differs from $y\,S_\chi(y)$ only by a factor of $n$:
$$S_\chi(y) = \sum_{n \le y} \Lambda(n)\chi(n)\Bigl(\frac{y}{n} - 1\Bigr)
= \int_1^y \Bigl(\sum_{n \le t} \frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n}\Bigr)\,dt.$$
Using $(y/n - 1)_+$ instead of $\mathbf{1}_{n \le y}$ buys two decisive advantages at zero cost:

1. **A removable singularity at $s = 1$ after linear subtraction.** Notice the factor $s - 1$ in
   the denominator $s(s - 1)$. If we left $-\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi)/(s(s-1))$ untouched for
   $\chi \neq 1$, it would have a spurious simple pole at $s = 1$ with residue
   $-\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)$ even though $L(1,\chi) \neq 0$! Adding the linear counterterm
   $+y\,\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)$ to $S_\chi(y)$ adds $\int_1^\infty y \frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi) y^{-s-1}\,dy = \frac{1}{s-1}\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi) = \frac{s}{s(s-1)}\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)$
   to the Mellin transform, turning it into
   $$-\frac{\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi) - s\,\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)}{s(s - 1)}.$$
   Now the numerator vanishes at $s = 1$, cancelling the factor $s - 1$ in the denominator! For
   $\zeta(s)$ ($N = 1, \chi = 1$), $-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s) = \frac{1}{s-1} - \gamma + O(s-1)$ near
   $s = 1$, so $-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)/(s(s-1)) = \frac{1}{(s-1)^2} - \frac{1+\gamma}{s-1} + O(1)$;
   subtracting $y \log y - (1 + \gamma)y$ (whose Mellin transform on $(1,\infty)$ is
   $\frac{1}{(s-1)^2} - \frac{1+\gamma}{s-1}$) cancels *both* singular terms at $s = 1$! For
   instance, at $y = 4$,
   $$S_\zeta(4) = \Lambda(2)\Bigl(\frac{4}{2}-1\Bigr) + \Lambda(3)\Bigl(\frac{4}{3}-1\Bigr) + \Lambda(4)\Bigl(\frac{4}{4}-1\Bigr)
   = \log 2 + \frac{\log 3}{3} \approx 1.0594,$$
   and $G_\zeta(4) = S_\zeta(4) - 4\log 4 + 4(1 + \gamma) \approx 1.0594 - 5.5452 + 6.3089 \approx 1.8230$
   (unrounded: $1.823036\dots$).
2. **Positive real residues at real zeros.** At any zero $\rho \in (0, 1)$ of $L(s,\chi)$ of order
   $m \ge 1$, we have $\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi) = \frac{m}{s - \rho} + O(1)$, so the Mellin transform
   has principal part $\frac{-m / (\rho(\rho - 1))}{s - \rho}$. Because $\rho(\rho - 1) < 0$ on
   $(0, 1)$, the residue $\frac{m}{\rho(1 - \rho)}$ is **strictly positive**!

### 3.2.2 Two complex poles and why vertical perturbation avoids Turán's lemma

Suppose a toy Laplace transform has no real poles, and two simple poles in $\{\Re s > \theta\}$ at
$\rho_\pm = \beta \pm i\tau$ ($\theta < \beta < 1$, $\tau > 0$) with conjugate residues $c, \bar c \neq 0$:
$$\Phi_{\mathrm{toy}}(s) = \frac{c}{s - (\beta + i\tau)} + \frac{\bar c}{s - (\beta - i\tau)},
\qquad g_{\mathrm{toy}}(u) = 2\Re\bigl(c\,e^{(\beta + i\tau)u}\bigr).$$
Fix a large real $\sigma > 2$ and examine the normalized Taylor coefficients
$$A_k(z) = \frac{(-1)^k \Phi_{\mathrm{toy}}^{(k)}(z)}{k!}
= \frac{c}{(z - \beta - i\tau)^{k+1}} + \frac{\bar c}{(z - \beta + i\tau)^{k+1}}.$$

- **If we foolishly stay on the real axis ($z = \sigma$):** both poles lie at the *same* distance
  $d_0 = \sqrt{(\sigma - \beta)^2 + \tau^2}$ from $\sigma$. First, $d_0$ can easily exceed
  $\sigma - \theta$ when $\tau$ is large (indeed $d_0 \le \sigma - \theta$ fails whenever
  $\tau^2 > (\sigma - \theta)^2 - (\sigma - \beta)^2$), so derivatives at $z = \sigma$ cannot see
  high-lying zeros at all! Second, even if $\tau$ is small so $d_0 < \sigma - \theta$, writing
  $\sigma - \beta - i\tau = d_0 e^{-i\alpha}$ gives $A_k(\sigma) = 2 d_0^{-k-1}\Re(c e^{i(k+1)\alpha})$,
  which **can vanish or become tiny** at the particular integer $k = \lceil(1 + \delta/2)\sigma U\rceil$
  forced upon us by the window $[U, (1+\delta)U]$!
- **If we shift vertically to $z = \sigma + i\tau$:** now $\rho_+ = \beta + i\tau$ is at distance
  $d = \lvert (\sigma + i\tau) - (\beta + i\tau) \rvert = \sigma - \beta < \sigma - \theta$, while
  $\rho_- = \beta - i\tau$ is strictly farther away, at distance $\sqrt{(\sigma - \beta)^2 + 4\tau^2} > d$:
  $$\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\tau) \rvert
  \ge \frac{\lvert c \rvert}{(\sigma - \beta)^{k+1}}
  - \frac{\lvert c \rvert}{\bigl((\sigma - \beta)^2 + 4\tau^2\bigr)^{(k+1)/2}}
  \sim \frac{\lvert c \rvert / (\sigma - \beta)}{(\sigma - \beta)^k} \gg \frac{1}{(\sigma - \theta)^k}.$$
- **What if the actual zero set $Z$ happens to contain *another* zero $\rho'$ at the exact same
  distance from $\sigma + i\tau$ as $\rho_+$?** For example, suppose $Z$ contained both
  $\beta + i(\tau + \delta_0)$ and $\beta + i(\tau - \delta_0)$, symmetric across height $\tau$.
  At $\gamma = \tau$, the two poles are equidistant from $\sigma + i\gamma$ and could cancel at a
  prescribed $k$! Classically one invokes P. Turán's power-sum method, varying $k$ across a block
  of consecutive integers to break the cancellation. The archive uses a much simpler geometric
  observation (`Deduction.landau_nearest`, `…/LandauTheorems/LandauNearest.lean:36`) [Lean, read]:
  any two distinct points $a, b \in \mathbb C$ in the half-plane $\Re s < \sigma$ have a unique
  perpendicular bisector, which can intersect the vertical line $\Re s = \sigma$ in **at most one
  point** $\sigma + i\gamma_{a,b}$ (and in zero points if $\Im a = \Im b$, where the bisector is
  vertical at $\frac{1}{2}(\Re a + \Re b) < \sigma$). Because $Z$ has only finitely many points in
  any compact disk inside $\{\Re s > 0\}$, only **finitely many** heights $\gamma$ near $\tau$ have
  tied nearest poles! Shifting $\gamma$ by an arbitrarily small $\varepsilon$ off that finite set
  guarantees a **strictly unique** nearest pole to $\sigma + i\gamma$, making Cauchy's elementary
  inequality do all the work of Turán's theorem at a *single* integer $k$!

One final detail in this toy model explains why `landau_window_ineq` bounds the *sum*
$\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert + \Re A_k(\sigma)$ rather than $\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert$
alone [Expository]. For $w_k(u) = \frac{u^k e^{-\sigma u}}{k!} \ge 0$, triangle inequality gives
$\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert \le \int_0^\infty \lvert g(u) \rvert w_k(u)\,du$, which involves
$\lvert g(u) \rvert$, whereas we only have an *upper* bound $g(u) \le R(u)$ ($R \ge 0$), not a
bound on $\lvert g(u) \rvert$! Observe, however, that for real $g(u)$,
$$\lvert g(u) \rvert + g(u) = \begin{cases} 2g(u) \le 2R(u) & \text{if } g(u) \ge 0,\\ 0 \le 2R(u) & \text{if } g(u) < 0. \end{cases}$$
Integrating against $w_k(u) \ge 0$ gives
$$\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert + \Re A_k(\sigma)
\le \int_0^\infty \bigl(\lvert g(u) \rvert + g(u)\bigr) w_k(u)\,du
\le 2\int_0^\infty R(u)\,w_k(u)\,du!$$
Could a large *negative* value of $\Re A_k(\sigma)$ hide a large value of $\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert$?
Only if $\Phi(s)$ had a singularity closer to $\sigma$ than $d = \lvert \sigma + i\gamma - \rho \rvert$!
If no pole of $\Phi$ lies in the open disk $B(\sigma, D)$ around $\sigma$ (with $d < D$), Cauchy's
estimate at $\sigma$ forces $\lvert \Re A_k(\sigma) \rvert \ll r'^{-k}$ ($d < r' < D$) to be
exponentially negligible compared to $d^{-k}$; and if a pole *does* hit the boundary of $B(\sigma, D)$
at minimal real part, elementary Euclidean geometry forces that pole to be **real** ($\beta = \sigma - D$),
where its positive real residue makes $\Re A_k(\sigma)$ huge and **positive**, contradicting the
inequality at $\gamma = 0$ directly!

## 3.3 Definitions

The archive develops the Landau-window criterion in two cleanly separated namespaces:
`Deduction` (`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/`), which proves the
analytic theorem for any finite admissible family of Dirichlet characters, and `Formalism.HC`
(`src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/`), which defines the
cyclic character levels consumed by the rest of the proof. The bridge file
`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/PrimitiveCosets/LevelFamily.lean` connects the two [Lean, read].

### 3.3.1 Riesz-weighted prime sums and normalized remainders

Let $\Lambda(n)$ denote the von Mangoldt function (`ArithmeticFunction.vonMangoldt`: $\Lambda(p^k) = \log p$
for prime powers $p^k \ge 2$, and $0$ otherwise), and let $\gamma$ denote the Euler–Mascheroni
constant (`Real.eulerMascheroniConstant`).

**Definition 3.1 (Riesz prime sum and normalized character remainder).** For any arithmetic
function $f : \mathbb N \to \mathbb C$ and real $y \in \mathbb R$, define the Riesz ramp sum
[Lean, read] (`Deduction.ramp`, `src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/CharFamily.lean:10`)
$$\operatorname{ramp}(f, y) := \sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor y \rfloor_{\mathbb N}} \Lambda(n)\,f(n)\,\max\Bigl(\frac{y}{n} - 1, 0\Bigr)$$
(where $\lfloor y \rfloor_{\mathbb N} = 0$ if $y < 0$, and the $n = 0$ term vanishes since $\Lambda(0) = 0$).
For $N \ge 1$ and $\chi : \mathrm{DirichletCharacter}\ \mathbb C\ N$, define the **normalized
Riesz remainder** $G_\chi(y)$ (`Deduction.Gchi`, `CharFamily.lean:15`) by
$$G_\chi(y) := \begin{cases}
\operatorname{ramp}(\chi, y) - y\log y + (1 + \gamma)\,y & \text{if }\chi = 1,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{ramp}(\chi, y) + y\,\dfrac{L'(1,\chi)}{L(1,\chi)} & \text{if }\chi \neq 1,
\end{cases}$$
and for the Riemann zeta function alone define the real-valued function (`Deduction.G`, `CharFamily.lean:21`)
$$G(y) := \Re\,\operatorname{ramp}(1, y) - y\log y + (1 + \gamma)\,y.$$

*(Compatibility across namespaces.)* Two other modules define the same mathematical objects with
slightly different summation domains (`Finset.Icc 1 ⌊y⌋₊` instead of `Finset.range (⌊y⌋₊ + 1)`,
and with or without `max · 0`, which is redundant when $1 \le n \le \lfloor y \rfloor_{\mathbb N} \le y$):
`Formalism.Sz` and `Formalism.Gz` (`src/Formalism/StatementOC.lean:11,13`), and `Formalism.HC.An.G`,
`An.sumLamChi`, `An.logDerivativeAtOne`, and `An.gChi`
(`src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/Analytic.lean:11,14,18,23`)
[Lean, read]. The archive proves their exact pointwise equality:
- `UpperTheory.Comparison₂.Gz_eq` (`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/PrimitiveCosets/GzEq.lean:6`)
  proves $\mathrm{Formalism.Gz}(y) = \mathrm{An.G}(y) = \mathrm{Deduction.G}(y)$ for all $y \in \mathbb R$ [Lean, read];
- `UpperTheory.Comparison₂.gChi_eq` (`…/PrimitiveCosets/GChiEq.lean:16`) proves
  $\mathrm{An.gChi}(\psi.\chi, y) = \mathrm{Deduction.Gchi}(\psi.\chi, y)$ for every primitive
  character $\psi : \mathrm{PChar}$ and every $y \in \mathbb R$ [Lean, read].
We can therefore write $G_\zeta(y)$ for $G(y)$ and $G_\chi(y)$ unambiguously throughout.

### 3.3.2 Admissible character families and window hypotheses

**Definition 3.2 (Admissible character family, `CharFamily`).** An **admissible character family**
$F$ (`Deduction.CharFamily`, `src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/CharFamily.lean:24`)
consists of [Lean, read]:
- a finite index type $\iota$;
- for each $i \in \iota$, a modulus $N_i \ge 1$, a Dirichlet character $\chi_i \bmod N_i$, and a
  real weight $a_i > 0$ (`a_pos`);
- the **admissibility condition** (`adm`): for every $i \in \iota$, either $N_i = 1$ or $\chi_i \neq 1$.

*(Why admissibility is required.)* If $N_i > 1$ and $\chi_i = 1 \bmod N_i$ were allowed, then
$L(s,\chi_i) = \zeta(s)\prod_{p \mid N_i}(1 - p^{-s})$ would have an asymptotic expansion near
$s = 1$ containing $\sum_{p \mid N_i} \frac{\log p}{p - 1}$ in addition to $\gamma$, so the
zeta counterterm $-y\log y + (1 + \gamma)y$ in Definition 3.1 would *not* cancel the simple pole
at $s = 1$! Requiring $N_i = 1 \lor \chi_i \neq 1$ ensures that every trivial character in $F$ is
literally $1 \bmod 1$ (giving $\zeta$), while every other character in $F$ is nontrivial (so
$L(s,\chi_i)$ is entire with $L(1,\chi_i) \neq 0$) [Expository]. Notice that every primitive
character $\psi : \mathrm{PChar}$ satisfies admissibility automatically: if $\psi.\chi = 1$, then
$N = \operatorname{cond}(\psi.\chi) = \operatorname{cond}(1) = 1$ (`level_family_adm`,
`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/PrimitiveCosets/LevelFamily.lean:19`) [Lean, read].

Associated to $F$ are the following functions and predicates [Lean, read] (`CharFamily.lean:36,38,41`;
`LandauZeroFreeCriterion.lean:9,11,25`):

1. **Weighted prime-sum remainder (`Deduction.Gm` and `Deduction.gm`):**
   $$G_m(F, y) := \sum_{i \in \iota} a_i\,\Re G_{\chi_i}(y), \qquad g_F(u) := G_m(F, e^u) \quad (y, u \in \mathbb R).$$
2. **Zero-freeness and symmetrized zero set (`ZeroFree` and `zeroSet`):** for $\theta \in \mathbb R$,
   $$\mathrm{ZeroFree}(F, \theta) \;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \forall i \in \iota,\ \forall s \in \mathbb C,\ \Re s > \theta \implies L(s,\chi_i) \neq 0,$$
   and
   $$Z(F) := \bigl\{\rho \in \mathbb C : \Re\rho > 0 \ \text{and}\ \exists i \in \iota,\ L(\rho,\chi_i) = 0 \ \text{or}\ L(\bar\rho,\chi_i) = 0\bigr\}.$$
3. **Integral window hypotheses (`WindowHyp` and `WindowHypU`):** for $\theta, \delta \in \mathbb R$,
   `WindowHyp F θ δ` holds if for every $\eta > 0$ and every $Y_0 \in \mathbb R$, there exist
   $Y \ge Y_0$ and a function $R : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ such that $y \mapsto R(y) y^{-\theta - 1}$
   is integrable on $[Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$,
   $$0 \le R(y) \quad\text{and}\quad G_m(F, y) \le R(y) \qquad \forall y \in [Y, Y^{1+\delta}],$$
   and
   $$\int_Y^{Y^{1+\delta}} R(y)\,y^{-\theta - 1}\,dy \le Y^\eta.$$
   In logarithmic coordinates $u = \log y$ ($U = \log Y$), `WindowHypU g θ δ` states identically
   that for every $\eta > 0$ and $U_0 \in \mathbb R$, there exist $U \ge U_0$ and $R : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$
   such that $u \mapsto R(u) e^{-\theta u}$ is integrable on $[U, (1+\delta)U]$, $0 \le R(u)$ and
   $g(u) \le R(u)$ on $[U, (1+\delta)U]$, and $\int_U^{(1+\delta)U} R(u) e^{-\theta u}\,du \le e^{\eta U}$.

### 3.3.3 Abstract Laplace-pole packages (`PoleData`)

Rather than tangling complex-analytic manipulations with Dirichlet series, the archive isolates the
analytic properties of $(g_F, \Phi, Z(F))$ into a clean seven-clause structure [Lean, read]
(`Deduction.PoleData`, `src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauZeroFreeCriterion.lean:15`):

**Definition 3.3 (`PoleData g Φ Z`).** Let $g : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$, $\Phi : \mathbb C \to \mathbb C$,
and $Z \subseteq \mathbb C$. We say `PoleData g Φ Z` holds if:

1. (`meas`) $g$ is Borel measurable;
2. (`growth`) there exists $C_0 \in \mathbb R$ such that $\lvert g(u) \rvert \le C_0 e^{2u}$ for all $u \ge 0$;
3. (`laplace`) for every $s \in \mathbb C$ with $\Re s > 2$, $\Phi(s) = \int_0^\infty g(u) e^{-su}\,du$;
4. (`pos`) every $\rho \in Z$ satisfies $\Re\rho > 0$;
5. (`diff`) $\Phi$ is complex-differentiable (hence holomorphic) on $\{s \in \mathbb C : \Re s > 0\} \setminus Z$;
6. (`iso`) $Z$ is discrete in $\{\Re s > 0\}$: for every $z$ with $\Re z > 0$, there is a punctured
   neighborhood of $z$ disjoint from $Z$;
7. (`pole`) at every $\rho \in Z$, $\Phi$ has a **simple pole** with nonzero residue $c \in \mathbb C \setminus \{0\}$
   — that is, $\Phi(s) = \frac{c}{s - \rho} + h(s)$ on a punctured neighborhood of $\rho$ for some
   function $h$ analytic at $\rho$ — and **if $\Im\rho = 0$, then $c$ is real and strictly positive**
   ($\Re c > 0$ and $\Im c = 0$).

### 3.3.4 Cyclic character levels (`Level` and `Level.cyc`)

In `Formalism.HC`, character families carry the extra group-theoretic structure needed by the
host-field Fourier analysis of Chapter 5 [Lean, read]
(`src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/PrimePairSizeCondition.lean:39`;
`…/ExplicitFormulaErrorOfHostConditions.lean:8,24–38,105,121`; `…/TotientGaloisClasses/Window.lean:7,30,33`):

**Definition 3.4 (`KSet`, `Level`, `Level.Gm`, and `Level.cyc`).**

1. A **`KSet`** consists of a finite set $\mathrm{KEPT}$ of bundled primitive characters
   (`Finset PChar`) containing $\mathrm{PChar.one}$ (`one_mem`) and closed under inversion
   $\psi \mapsto \psi.\mathrm{inv}$ (`inv_mem`), together with an optional distinguished character
   $\mathrm{psK} : \mathrm{Option}\ \mathrm{PChar}$ which, when present ($\mathrm{psK} = \mathrm{some}\ \psi_0$),
   belongs to $\mathrm{KEPT}$ and is odd and real ($\psi_0.\chi(-1) = -1$ and $\Im \psi_0.\chi(a) = 0$
   for all $a$). (In Chapters 4–5, $\mathrm{psK}$ tracks a possible quadratic character coming
   from the host frame; for the cyclic levels used in the endgame below, $\mathrm{psK} = \mathrm{none}$.)
2. A **`Level`** $\Lambda$ extends `KSet` by a natural-number parameter $o = \Lambda.o \in \mathbb N$
   (recording the subgroup order). Writing $\Lambda.\mathrm{Nt} := \Lambda.\mathrm{KEPT} \setminus \{\mathrm{PChar.one}\}$,
   each nontrivial character $\psi \in \Lambda.\mathrm{Nt}$ receives the positive weight
   $$\Lambda.\mathrm{wt}(\psi) := \begin{cases}
   1 & \text{if } \Lambda.\mathrm{psK} = \mathrm{some}\ \psi,\\
   (3o + 1)^{-1} & \text{otherwise},
   \end{cases}$$
   and the level's prime-sum function is
   $$\Lambda.\mathrm{Gm}(y) := G_\zeta(y) + \sum_{\psi \in \Lambda.\mathrm{Nt}} \Lambda.\mathrm{wt}(\psi)\,\Re G_{\psi.\chi}(y).$$
3. Given any subgroup $\Xi \le \mathrm{DirichletCharacter}\ \mathbb C\ M$ ($M \ge 1$), `Level.ofSub Ξ`
   sets $\mathrm{KEPT}$ to be the image under $\mathrm{PChar.of}$ of $\Xi$ (the primitive
   characters underlying all characters in $\Xi$), sets $\mathrm{psK} = \mathrm{none}$, and sets
   $o = \lvert \Xi \rvert$. For a primitive character $\psi : \mathrm{PChar}$, the **cyclic level**
   `Level.cyc ψ` is `Level.ofSub (Subgroup.zpowers ψ.χ)`, generated by the powers $(\psi.\chi)^m$
   ($m \in \mathbb Z$) modulo $\psi.N$. Since $\psi$ is primitive, $\mathrm{PChar.of}(\psi.\chi) = \psi$
   (`PChar.of_self`, `:59`), so $\psi \in (\mathrm{Level.cyc}\ \psi).\mathrm{KEPT}$ (`Level.mem_cyc`, `:123`).
4. For a real scale exponent $\lambda > 1$ (written `la` in the code) and a set $S \subseteq \mathbb R$,
   `HasWindows S la` means: for every $y_0 \in \mathbb R$, there exists $Y \ge y_0$ such that
   $[Y, Y^\lambda] \subseteq S$. The level's localized Landau property `Λ.FormBAt la` asserts that
   for every $\theta \in (0, 1)$,
   $$\mathrm{HasWindows}\bigl(\{x \in \mathbb R : \Lambda.\mathrm{Gm}(x) \le x^\theta\},\,\lambda\bigr)
   \implies \forall \psi \in \Lambda.\mathrm{KEPT},\ \forall s \in \mathbb C,\ \Re s > \theta \implies L(s, \psi.\chi) \neq 0,$$
   and `Λ.FormB` asserts `∀ la > 1, Λ.FormBAt la`.

### 3.4.1 Mellin transforms and realization of `PoleData`

**Proposition 3.1 (Quadratic growth and Mellin/Laplace identities for $G_\chi$).** *Let $N \ge 1$
and let $\chi \bmod N$ satisfy the admissibility condition $N = 1 \lor \chi \neq 1$.*

1. *(Crude quadratic bound.) There exists a constant $C_\chi > 0$ such that
   $\lvert G_\chi(y) \rvert \le C_\chi y^2$ for all $y \ge 1$.*
2. *(Mellin transform on $\Re s > 1$.) If $\chi \neq 1$, then for every $s \in \mathbb C$ with
   $\Re s > 1$,*
   $$\int_1^\infty G_\chi(y)\,y^{-s-1}\,dy
   = -\frac{\dfrac{L'(s,\chi)}{L(s,\chi)} - s\,\dfrac{L'(1,\chi)}{L(1,\chi)}}{s(s - 1)}.$$
   *If $N = 1$ (so $\chi = 1 \bmod 1$ and $L(s,\chi) = \zeta(s)$), then for every $s \in \mathbb C$
   with $\Re s > 1$,*
   $$\int_1^\infty G_\chi(y)\,y^{-s-1}\,dy
   = -\frac{\dfrac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)} + \dfrac{1}{s - 1} + 1 - (1 + \gamma)s}{s(s - 1)}.$$
3. *(Laplace transform on $\Re s > 2$.) Substituting $y = e^u$ ($u > 0$, $dy = e^u\,du$) identifies
   the absolutely convergent Laplace integral $\int_0^\infty G_\chi(e^u) e^{-su}\,du$ on $\Re s > 2$
   with the Mellin integral $\int_1^\infty G_\chi(y) y^{-s-1}\,dy$.*

*(Lean: `Gchi_growth`, `src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/GchiGrowth.lean:37`;
`mellin_ramp`, `…/MellinRamp.lean:10`; `mellin_Gchi_of_ne_one`, `…/mellinGchiOfNeOne.lean:116`;
`mellin_Gchi_modOne`, `…/mellinGchiModOne.lean:115`; `poleData_integrable` and `poleData_change`,
`…/poleData.lean:91,114`.)* [Lean, read]

*Proof.*
(1) For $y \ge 1$ and $1 \le n \le \lfloor y \rfloor$, we have $0 \le \Lambda(n) \le \log n \le n$,
$\lvert \chi(n) \rvert \le 1$, and $0 \le \max(y/n - 1, 0) \le y/n$. Thus each summand of
$\operatorname{ramp}(\chi, y)$ has modulus at most $n \cdot 1 \cdot (y/n) = y$ (and the $n = 0$
term is $0$). Since there are $\lfloor y \rfloor + 1 \le 2y$ terms, $\lvert \operatorname{ramp}(\chi, y) \rvert \le 2y^2$.
For $y \ge 1$, both $y\log y \le y^2$ and $y \le y^2$, so adding the normalization terms gives
$\lvert G_\chi(y) \rvert \le C_\chi y^2$ with $C_\chi = 3 + \lvert 1 + \gamma \rvert$ when $\chi = 1$
and $C_\chi = 2 + \lvert L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi) \rvert$ when $\chi \neq 1$.

(2) For each integer $n \ge 1$ and $\Re s > 1$, we already computed in Section 3.2.1 that
$$\int_1^\infty \max\Bigl(\frac{y}{n} - 1, 0\Bigr) y^{-s-1}\,dy
= \int_n^\infty \Bigl(\frac{1}{n} y^{-s} - y^{-s-1}\Bigr)\,dy = \frac{n^{-s}}{s(s - 1)},$$
and taking $\sigma_0 = \Re s > 1$ shows that the $L^1$-norm of the $n$-th term $\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\max(y/n - 1, 0)y^{-s-1}$
over $(1, \infty)$ is bounded by $\Lambda(n) n^{-\sigma_0} / (\sigma_0(\sigma_0 - 1))$, which is
summable over $n \ge 1$ because $-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma_0) = \sum_{n \ge 1} \Lambda(n) n^{-\sigma_0} < \infty$.
By Fubini–Tonelli (`hasSum_integral_of_summable_integral_norm`), summation over $n$ commutes with
integration over $(1, \infty)$, yielding
$$\int_1^\infty \operatorname{ramp}(\chi, y)\,y^{-s-1}\,dy
= \frac{1}{s(s - 1)} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^s}
= -\frac{L'(s,\chi)/L(s,\chi)}{s(s - 1)}$$
(with $\zeta$ in place of $L(\cdot, \chi)$ when $N = 1$). Now add the Mellin transforms of the
counterterms on $(1, \infty)$ for $\Re s > 1$:
$$\int_1^\infty y \cdot y^{-s-1}\,dy = \int_1^\infty y^{-s}\,dy = \frac{1}{s - 1} = \frac{s}{s(s - 1)},$$
and, integrating $\frac{d}{dy}\bigl(y^{1-s}\log y\bigr) = (1 - s)y^{-s}\log y + y^{-s}$ over
$[1, \infty)$ (where $y^{1-s}\log y \to 0$ as $y \to \infty$ since $\Re s > 1$, and $\log 1 = 0$),
$$\int_1^\infty (y\log y)\,y^{-s-1}\,dy = \int_1^\infty y^{-s}\log y\,dy = \frac{1}{(s - 1)^2} = \frac{s/(s - 1)}{s(s - 1)}.$$
When $\chi \neq 1$, adding $\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi)\cdot \frac{s}{s(s-1)}$ gives the first formula.
When $N = 1$, subtracting $\frac{1}{(s-1)^2}$ and adding $(1 + \gamma)\frac{s}{s(s-1)}$ produces
$$-\frac{\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)}{s(s - 1)} - \frac{1}{(s - 1)^2} + \frac{(1 + \gamma)s}{s(s - 1)}
= -\frac{\dfrac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)} + \dfrac{s}{s - 1} - (1 + \gamma)s}{s(s - 1)},$$
and writing $\frac{s}{s - 1} = \frac{1}{s - 1} + 1$ gives the second formula.

(3) By (1), for $u > 0$ we have $\lvert G_\chi(e^u) e^{-su} \rvert \le C_\chi e^{2u} e^{-(\Re s)u} = C_\chi e^{-(\Re s - 2)u}$,
which is integrable on $(0, \infty)$ whenever $\Re s > 2$. Since $u \mapsto e^u$ is a smooth
strictly increasing bijection $(0, \infty) \xrightarrow{\sim} (1, \infty)$ with derivative $e^u$,
the change-of-variables theorem gives
$$\int_0^\infty G_\chi(e^u)\,e^{-su}\,du
= \int_0^\infty G_\chi(e^u)\,(e^u)^{-s-1}\,e^u\,du
= \int_1^\infty G_\chi(y)\,y^{-s-1}\,dy. \qquad \square$$

Now we extend the right-hand side meromorphically to $\Re s > 0$ and verify all seven clauses of
`PoleData`.

**Theorem 3.2 (Realization of `PoleData` for any admissible character family).** *For every
admissible character family $F : \mathrm{CharFamily}$, there exists a function
$\Phi : \mathbb C \to \mathbb C$ satisfying `PoleData (gm F) Φ (zeroSet F)`.*
*(Lean: `poleData_abstract`, `poleData_char`, and `poleData`,
`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/poleData.lean:22,137,248`;
with `logDeriv_local`, `…/LogDerivLocal.lean:9`; `LFunction_conj`, `…/LFunctionConj.lean:88`;
`Gchi_conj`, `…/GchiConj.lean:38`; `measurable_Gchi`, `…/measurableGchi.lean:24`; and
`charFamily_LFunction_ne_zero_of_one_le_re`, `…/CharFamilyLFunctionNeZeroOfOneLeRe.lean:9`.)* [Lean, read]

*Proof.* We proceed in three steps, matching `poleData_abstract` $\to$ `poleData_char` $\to$ `poleData`.

**Step 1: One entire function (`poleData_abstract`).** Let $g : \mathbb C \to \mathbb C$ be any
entire function with $g(1) \neq 0$, and let $k \in \mathbb C$ be a constant. Set $c_1 = g'(1)/g(1)$
and consider
$$N(s) := \frac{g'(s)}{g(s)} - s\,c_1 + k(1 - s)$$
on the open set $U = \{s \in \mathbb C : g(s) \neq 0\}$. Since $g$ is entire and $1 \in U$, $N$ is
holomorphic on $U$ and satisfies $N(1) = c_1 - c_1 + 0 = 0$. Therefore the difference quotient
$$Q(s) := \begin{cases}
\dfrac{N(s) - N(1)}{s - 1} = \dfrac{N(s)}{s - 1} & (s \neq 1),\\[6pt]
N'(1) & (s = 1)
\end{cases}$$
(`dslope N 1` in Mathlib) is holomorphic on the *entire* open set $U$ — including at $s = 1$! Define
$\Phi_g(s) := -Q(s)/s$. Then:
- For every $s \neq 1$, $\Phi_g(s) = -\dfrac{g'(s)/g(s) - s(g'(1)/g(1)) + k(1 - s)}{s(s - 1)}$.
- At every $\rho \in \mathbb C \setminus \{0\}$ with $g(\rho) \neq 0$, $\Phi_g$ is analytic at $\rho$.
- Because $g$ is entire and not identically zero ($g(1) \neq 0$), its zeros are isolated; at any
  $\rho \neq 0$, write $g(s) = (s - \rho)^m g_0(s)$ near $\rho$ with $m = \operatorname{ord}_{s=\rho} g \ge 0$
  ($m = 0 \iff g(\rho) \neq 0$) and $g_0(\rho) \neq 0$ (`logDeriv_local`). If $m \ge 1$, then
  $\rho \neq 1$ (since $g(1) \neq 0$) and $\frac{g'}{g}(s) = \frac{m}{s - \rho} + h_0(s)$ with
  $h_0 = g_0'/g_0$ analytic at $\rho$. Writing $\frac{1}{s(s-1)} = \frac{1}{\rho(\rho-1)} + (s - \rho)q(s)$
  with $q$ analytic at $\rho$ (since $\rho \neq 0, 1$), we obtain on a punctured neighborhood of $\rho$:
  $$\Phi_g(s) = -\Bigl(\frac{m}{s - \rho} + h_0(s) - s c_1 + k(1 - s)\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1}{\rho(\rho - 1)} + (s - \rho)q(s)\Bigr)
  = \frac{m \cdot \bigl(-1 / (\rho(\rho - 1))\bigr)}{s - \rho} + h(s),$$
  where $h(s) = -m\,q(s) - (h_0(s) - s c_1 + k(1 - s))/(s(s - 1))$ is analytic at $\rho$. (If $m = 0$,
  the same formula holds trivially with $h = \Phi_g$.)

**Step 2: Single admissible character (`poleData_char`).** Let $\chi \bmod N$ satisfy $N = 1 \lor \chi \neq 1$.
- If $\chi \neq 1$, then $g(s) = L(s,\chi)$ is entire (`differentiable_LFunction`) and satisfies
  $L(1,\chi) \neq 0$ (`LFunction_apply_one_ne_zero`, `Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/Nonvanishing.lean:406`).
  Applying Step 1 with $k = 0$ produces $\Phi_\chi := \Phi_g$, which for $\Re s > 2$ agrees with
  $\int_0^\infty G_\chi(e^u) e^{-su}\,du$ by Proposition 3.1(2)–(3).
- If $\chi = 1$, admissibility forces $N = 1$, so $L(s,\chi) = \zeta(s)$. Define
  $$g(s) := \begin{cases} (s - 1)\zeta(s) & (s \neq 1),\\ 1 & (s = 1). \end{cases}$$
  Since $\lim_{s \to 1} (s - 1)\zeta(s) = 1$ (`riemannZeta_residue_one`), Riemann's removable
  singularity theorem shows that $g$ is entire with $g(1) = 1 \neq 0$. Moreover, the classical
  Laurent expansion $\zeta(s) = \frac{1}{s - 1} + \gamma + o(1)$ as $s \to 1$
  (`tendsto_riemannZeta_sub_one_div`) gives
  $$g'(1) = \lim_{s \to 1} \frac{(s - 1)\zeta(s) - 1}{s - 1}
  = \lim_{s \to 1} \Bigl(\zeta(s) - \frac{1}{s - 1}\Bigr) = \gamma,$$
  while for $s \neq 1$ the product rule gives $\frac{g'(s)}{g(s)} = \frac{1}{s - 1} + \frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}$
  whenever $\zeta(s) \neq 0$ (and $g(w) = 0 \iff \zeta(w) = 0$ for all $w \in \mathbb C$, since
  $g(1) = 1 \neq 0$ and $\zeta(1) \neq 0$ in Mathlib). Applying Step 1 to $g$ with $k = 1$ yields
  for $s \neq 1$:
  $$\Phi_g(s) = -\frac{\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)} + \frac{1}{s - 1} - \gamma s + (1 - s)}{s(s - 1)}
  = -\frac{\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)} + \frac{1}{s - 1} + 1 - (1 + \gamma)s}{s(s - 1)},$$
  which matches Proposition 3.1(2)–(3) identically!

**Step 3: Symmetrization and positive sum (`poleData`).** Given an admissible family
$F = (\iota, N_i, \chi_i, a_i)$, each inverse character $\chi_i^{-1}$ is also admissible
($\chi_i \neq 1 \implies \chi_i^{-1} \neq 1$). Furthermore, complex conjugation satisfies
$\overline{L(s, \chi)} = L(\bar s, \chi^{-1})$ for all $s \in \mathbb C$ (`LFunction_conj`: proved
on $\Re s > 1$ from the Hurwitz/Dirichlet series and extended to $\mathbb C$ by analytic
continuation of the regularized Hurwitz zeta function!), whence $\overline{L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)} = L'(1,\chi^{-1})/L(1,\chi^{-1})$
and therefore $\overline{G_\chi(y)} = G_{\chi^{-1}}(y)$ for all $y \in \mathbb R$ (`Gchi_conj`).
Consequently,
$$g_F(u) = \sum_{i \in \iota} a_i\,\Re G_{\chi_i}(e^u)
= \sum_{i \in \iota} \frac{a_i}{2}\bigl(G_{\chi_i}(e^u) + G_{\chi_i^{-1}}(e^u)\bigr).$$
Let $\Phi_{1,i}$ and $\Phi_{2,i}$ be the meromorphic functions produced by Step 2 for $\chi_i$ and
$\chi_i^{-1}$, and define
$$\Phi(s) := \sum_{i \in \iota} \frac{a_i}{2}\bigl(\Phi_{1,i}(s) + \Phi_{2,i}(s)\bigr).$$
Notice that $L(\bar w, \chi_i) = 0 \iff \overline{L(\bar w, \chi_i)} = 0 \iff L(w, \chi_i^{-1}) = 0$,
so a point $w$ with $\Re w > 0$ belongs to $Z(F)$ if and only if $L(w, \chi_i) = 0$ or
$L(w, \chi_i^{-1}) = 0$ for some $i \in \iota$. We verify the seven clauses of `PoleData`:
1. `meas`: Each $y \mapsto G_{\chi_i}(y)$ is measurable (`measurable_Gchi`, since $y \mapsto \lfloor y \rfloor_{\mathbb N}$
   is measurable), so $g_F$ is measurable.
2. `growth`: By Proposition 3.1(1), for $u \ge 0$ ($e^u \ge 1$), $\lvert g_F(u) \rvert \le \bigl(\sum_i a_i C_{\chi_i}\bigr) e^{2u}$.
3. `laplace`: Linearity of the integral on $\Re s > 2$ gives $\int_0^\infty g_F(u) e^{-su}\,du = \Phi(s)$.
4. `pos`: By definition of $Z(F)$, $\Re\rho > 0$ for all $\rho \in Z(F)$.
5. `diff`: On $\{\Re s > 0\} \setminus Z(F)$, we have $s \neq 0$ and $L(s,\chi_i) \neq 0$,
   $L(s,\chi_i^{-1}) \neq 0$ for all $i$, so every $\Phi_{1,i}, \Phi_{2,i}$ is analytic at $s$.
6. `iso`: Finite unions of locally finite zero sets remain locally finite.
7. `pole`: Fix $\rho \in Z(F)$, so $\Re\rho > 0$ (hence $\rho \neq 0$). By classical non-vanishing
   on $\Re s \ge 1$ (`charFamily_LFunction_ne_zero_of_one_le_re`, which invokes Mathlib's
   `riemannZeta_ne_zero_of_one_le_re` when $N_i = 1$ and `LFunction_ne_zero_of_one_le_re` when
   $\chi_i \neq 1$), no character in $F$ or its inverse can vanish on $\Re s \ge 1$; thus
   $\Re\rho < 1$, and in particular $\rho \neq 1$. Let $m_{1,i} = \operatorname{ord}_{s=\rho} L(s,\chi_i)$
   and $m_{2,i} = \operatorname{ord}_{s=\rho} L(s,\chi_i^{-1})$ (using $\zeta$ when $N_i = 1$). By Step 2,
   near $\rho$ we have $\Phi(s) = \frac{c_\rho}{s - \rho} + h(s)$ with $h$ analytic at $\rho$ and
   $$c_\rho = S_\rho \cdot \Bigl(-\frac{1}{\rho(\rho - 1)}\Bigr), \qquad
   S_\rho := \sum_{i \in \iota} \frac{a_i}{2}\bigl(m_{1,i} + m_{2,i}\bigr).$$
   Since $\rho \in Z(F)$, at least one $i_0 \in \iota$ has $m_{1,i_0} \ge 1$ or $m_{2,i_0} \ge 1$.
   Because $a_i > 0$ and $m_{1,i}, m_{2,i} \ge 0$ for **every** $i \in \iota$, we have
   $$S_\rho \ge \frac{a_{i_0}}{2} \cdot 1 > 0!$$
   Since $\rho \notin \{0, 1\}$, we have $-1 / (\rho(\rho - 1)) \neq 0$, so $c_\rho \neq 0$. Finally,
   suppose $\Im\rho = 0$, so $\rho = \beta \in \mathbb R$. Because $0 < \Re\rho < 1$, we have
   $0 < \beta < 1$, whence $\beta(\beta - 1) < 0$ and $-1 / (\beta(\beta - 1)) > 0$. Multiplying by
   $S_\rho > 0$ shows that $c_\beta$ is real (`c.im = 0`) and strictly positive (`0 < c.re`). $\square$

### 3.4.2 From pointwise power windows to logarithmic integral windows

Before proving `landau_window`, we record the elementary translation from pointwise bounds
$G_m(F, y) \le C y^\theta$ on $[Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$ to `WindowHyp` and `WindowHypU`.

**Lemma 3.3 (Pointwise $y^\theta$-windows imply `WindowHyp` and `WindowHypU`).** *Let $F : \mathrm{CharFamily}$,
$\theta \in \mathbb R$, $\delta > 0$, and $C \in \mathbb R$. Suppose that for every $Y_0 \in \mathbb R$
there exists $Y \ge Y_0$ such that $G_m(F, y) \le C y^\theta$ for all $y \in [Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$.
Then `WindowHyp F θ δ` holds, and hence `WindowHypU (gm F) θ δ` holds.*
*(Lean: `windowHyp_of_rpow`, `src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/WindowHypOfRpow.lean:27`;
`windowHypU_of_windowHyp`, `…/WindowHypUOfWindowHyp.lean:29`.)* [Lean, read]

*Proof.* Fix $\eta > 0$ and $Y_0 \in \mathbb R$. Since $\lim_{Y \to \infty} Y^{\eta/2} = \infty$
and $\log Y \le \frac{Y^{\eta/2}}{\eta / 2}$ for all $Y > 0$, there exists $Y_1 \ge 1$ such that
for all $Y \ge Y_1$, we have $Y \ge 1$ and $\lvert C \rvert \delta \log Y \le Y^\eta$
(`windowHyp_of_rpow_eventually`: choose $Y_1 \ge 1$ so that $Y^{\eta/2} \ge \frac{\lvert C \rvert \delta}{\eta / 2}$
for $Y \ge Y_1$, giving $\lvert C \rvert \delta \log Y \le \frac{\lvert C \rvert \delta}{\eta/2} Y^{\eta/2} \le Y^\eta$).

Apply the hypothesis to $\max(Y_0, Y_1)$ to get $Y \ge \max(Y_0, Y_1) \ge 1$ on which
$G_m(F, y) \le C y^\theta$ for all $y \in [Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$. Define the majorant
$R(y) := \lvert C \rvert y^\theta$. Since $Y \ge 1$ and $\delta > 0$, for every $y \in [Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$
we have $y \ge 1 > 0$, so $R(y) \ge 0$ and $G_m(F, y) \le C y^\theta \le \lvert C \rvert y^\theta = R(y)$.
Moreover, on $[Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$ the integrand simplifies to
$$R(y)\,y^{-\theta - 1} = \lvert C \rvert y^\theta y^{-\theta - 1} = \frac{\lvert C \rvert}{y},$$
which is continuous on $[Y, Y^{1+\delta}] \subset (0, \infty)$ and has exact integral
$$\int_Y^{Y^{1+\delta}} \frac{\lvert C \rvert}{y}\,dy
= \lvert C \rvert \bigl(\log(Y^{1+\delta}) - \log Y\bigr)
= \lvert C \rvert \delta \log Y \le Y^\eta.$$
This establishes `WindowHyp F θ δ`.

Finally, given $\eta > 0$ and $U_0 \in \mathbb R$, apply `WindowHyp F θ δ` at $Y_0 = e^{U_0}$ to
obtain $Y \ge e^{U_0} > 0$ and $R$, and set $U := \log Y \ge U_0$ and $\widetilde R(u) := R(e^u)$.
The diffeomorphism $u \mapsto e^u$ maps $[U, (1+\delta)U]$ onto $[Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$ with Jacobian
$e^u$, transforming $\widetilde R(u) e^{-\theta u}\,du = R(e^u) (e^u)^{-\theta - 1} e^u\,du$ into
$R(y) y^{-\theta - 1}\,dy$ and bounding the integral by $Y^\eta = e^{\eta U}$. This gives
`WindowHypU (gm F) θ δ`. $\square$

### 3.4.3 One-sided kernel inequality and tail concentration

We now enter the proof of `landau_window`, working solely from the abstract hypotheses
`PoleData g Φ Z` and `WindowHypU g θ δ`. For any $k \in \mathbb N$ and $s \in \mathbb C$, write
$$A_k(s) := \frac{(-1)^k \Phi^{(k)}(s)}{k!}$$
for the $k$-th power-series jet coefficient of $\Phi$ at $s$ (with sign $(-1)^k$).

**Lemma 3.4 (Upper bound on jet coefficients from a one-sided window; `landau_window_key`).**
*Assume `PoleData g Φ Z`, $0 < \theta < 1$, $\delta > 0$, and `WindowHypU g θ δ`. Then there exist
an abscissa $\sigma > 2$ (depending only on $\delta$) and a constant $C > 0$ such that for every
$\eta > 0$ and every $U_0 \in \mathbb R$, there exist $U \ge U_0$ and an integer $k \ge U$
satisfying, for **every** $\gamma \in \mathbb R$,*
$$\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert + \Re A_k(\sigma) \le \frac{C\,e^{\eta U}}{(\sigma - \theta)^k}.$$
*(Lean: `landau_coeff`, `…/LandauCoeff.lean:10`; `iteratedDeriv_laplace`, `…/IteratedDerivLaplace.lean:84`;
`landau_kernel_max`, `…/LandauKernelMax.lean:9`; `landau_window_ineq`, `…/landauWindowIneq.lean:27`;
`kernel_integral`, `…/kernelIntegral.lean:9`; `landau_tails`, `…/landauTails.lean:80`; and
`landau_window_key`, `…/landauWindow.lean:40`.)* [Lean, read]

*Proof.* We break the estimate into three self-contained steps.

**Step 1: Integral formula and one-sided window splitting (`landau_coeff` and `landau_window_ineq`).**
By `PoleData.growth`, $\lvert g(u) \rvert \le C_0 e^{2u}$ for all $u \ge 0$ (and evaluating at
$u = 0$ gives $C_0 \ge \lvert g(0) \rvert \ge 0$). On the open half-plane $\Re s > 2$, locally
uniform domination of $\lvert g(u)(-u)^{j} e^{-zu} \rvert \le C_0 u^j e^{-\delta_s u}$ ($\delta_s = (\Re s - 2)/2 > 0$)
justifies differentiating $\Phi(s) = \int_0^\infty g(u) e^{-su}\,du$ repeatedly under the integral
sign (`iteratedDeriv_laplace`), giving
$$A_k(s) = \frac{(-1)^k}{k!} \int_0^\infty g(u)\,(-u)^k e^{-su}\,du
= \int_0^\infty g(u)\,\frac{u^k e^{-su}}{k!}\,du \qquad (\Re s > 2).$$

Fix $\sigma > 2$ (so $\theta < 1 < 2 < \sigma$), any window $[U, V] \subset (0, \infty)$ ($U > 0$)
on which $0 \le R(u)$ and $g(u) \le R(u)$, any $k \in \mathbb N$, and any $\gamma \in \mathbb R$.
Set $w_k(u) := \frac{u^k e^{-\sigma u}}{k!} \ge 0$. Since $\lvert e^{-(\sigma + i\gamma)u} \rvert = e^{-\sigma u}$,
the triangle inequality gives $\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert \le \int_0^\infty \lvert g(u) \rvert w_k(u)\,du$,
while $g$ and $w_k$ are real-valued so $\Re A_k(\sigma) = \int_0^\infty g(u) w_k(u)\,du$. Adding
the two and splitting $(0, \infty)$ into $[U, V]$ and its complement $T := (0, \infty) \setminus [U, V]$ yields
$$\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert + \Re A_k(\sigma)
\le \int_U^V \bigl(\lvert g(u) \rvert + g(u)\bigr) w_k(u)\,du
+ \int_T \bigl(\lvert g(u) \rvert + g(u)\bigr) w_k(u)\,du.$$
- **Inside $[U, V]$:** Since $R(u) \ge 0$ and $g(u) \le R(u)$, we have $\lvert g(u) \rvert + g(u) = \max(2g(u), 0) \le 2R(u)$.
  Moreover, for any $a > 0$ and $u \ge 0$, the elementary bound $\frac{(au)^k}{k!} \le e^{au}$
  implies $\frac{u^k e^{-au}}{k!} \le \frac{1}{a^k}$ (`landau_kernel_max`). Applying this with
  $a = \sigma - \theta > 0$ gives
  $$w_k(u) = \Bigl(\frac{u^k e^{-(\sigma - \theta)u}}{k!}\Bigr) e^{-\theta u}
  \le \frac{1}{(\sigma - \theta)^k}\,e^{-\theta u},$$
  so the window integral is at most $\frac{2}{(\sigma - \theta)^k} \int_U^V R(u) e^{-\theta u}\,du$.
- **Outside $[U, V]$:** For every $u > 0$, $\lvert g(u) \rvert + g(u) \le 2\lvert g(u) \rvert \le 2 C_0 e^{2u}$,
  and $e^{2u} w_k(u) = \frac{u^k e^{-(\sigma - 2)u}}{k!}$.

Combining both pieces gives `landau_window_ineq`:
$$\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert + \Re A_k(\sigma)
\le \frac{2}{(\sigma - \theta)^k}\int_U^V R(u)\,e^{-\theta u}\,du
+ 2 C_0 \int_{(0,\infty) \setminus [U, V]} \frac{u^k e^{-(\sigma - 2)u}}{k!}\,du.$$

**Step 2: Choosing $\sigma$ and $k$ to crush both tails (`landau_tails`).**
Now set $V = (1 + \delta)U$ with $U \ge 1$. We want to pick $\sigma > 2$ and a constant $C_1 > 0$
(both depending *only* on $\delta$), and for each $U \ge 1$ an integer $k \ge U$, so that
$$\int_{(0,\infty) \setminus [U, (1+\delta)U]} \frac{u^k e^{-(\sigma - 2)u}}{k!}\,du \le \frac{C_1}{\sigma^k}.$$

First recall the exact Gamma integral (`kernel_integral`): for any $a > 0$,
$$\int_0^\infty \frac{u^k e^{-au}}{k!}\,du = \frac{1}{a^{k+1}}.$$
Next apply a Chernoff / exponential-tilting bound (`landau_tails_chernoff`): for any $s_0 > l \ge 0$
and any $u \in (0, \infty) \setminus [U, V]$, either $u < U$ (so $l(U - u) > 0$ and
$e^{-s_0 u} \le e^{l U} e^{-(s_0 + l)u}$) or $u > V$ (so $l(u - V) > 0$ and
$e^{-s_0 u} \le e^{-l V} e^{-(s_0 - l)u}$). In either case,
$$e^{-s_0 u} \le e^{-l V} e^{-(s_0 - l)u} + e^{l U} e^{-(s_0 + l)u} \qquad \forall u \in (0,\infty)\setminus[U,V].$$
Multiplying by $u^k / k! \ge 0$, extending both integrals to all of $(0, \infty)$, and applying
$\int_0^\infty \frac{u^k e^{-au}}{k!}\,du = a^{-(k+1)}$ gives, with $s_0 = \sigma - 2$:
$$\int_{(0,\infty)\setminus[U,V]} \frac{u^k e^{-(\sigma - 2)u}}{k!}\,du
\le \frac{e^{-l V}}{(\sigma - 2 - l)^{k+1}} + \frac{e^{l U}}{(\sigma - 2 + l)^{k+1}}$$
whenever $0 \le l < \sigma - 2$.

To bound an expression of the form $e^x a^{-(k+1)}$ by $\frac{e^c}{a\,\sigma^k}$ (`landau_tails_pow`),
rewrite $e^x a^{-(k+1)} = \frac{1}{a\,\sigma^k} e^x (\sigma / a)^k$ and use $1 + t \le e^t$ at
$t = (\sigma - a)/a$ to get $(\sigma / a)^k \le \exp\bigl(k\,\frac{\sigma - a}{a}\bigr)$; thus it
suffices that $x + k\,\frac{\sigma - a}{a} \le c$. Let us check the exact parameters chosen in
`landauTails.lean:83–98`:
$$l := 2 + \frac{6}{\delta} > 2, \qquad M := l(1 + \delta), \qquad \sigma := M(2 + l), \qquad
k := \bigl\lceil (1 + \tfrac{\delta}{2})\,\sigma\,U \bigr\rceil.$$
Notice first that $l\delta = 2\delta + 6$, which yields two exact algebraic identities:
- $(1 + \frac{\delta}{2})(l - 2) = (1 + \frac{\delta}{2})\frac{6}{\delta} = \frac{6}{\delta} + 3 = l + 1$;
- $(1 + \frac{\delta}{2})(2 + l) = 2 + l + \delta + \frac{l\delta}{2} = 2 + l + \delta + (\delta + 3) = l + 2\delta + 5 = l + l\delta - 1 = M - 1$,
  and multiplying by $M$ gives
  $$\bigl(1 + \tfrac{\delta}{2}\bigr)\sigma = M(M - 1).$$
Moreover, since $l > 2$ and $M > l > 2$, we have $\sigma = M(2 + l) > l(2 + l) > l + 2$, so
$\sigma > 2$, $M - 1 > 1$, and $a_- := \sigma - 2 - l > 0$, $a_+ := \sigma - 2 + l > 0$, while for
$U \ge 1$ clearly $k \ge (1 + \delta/2)\sigma U \ge U$. Now check both tails:
1. **Right tail ($x = -l V = -l(1+\delta)U = -M U$, $a = a_- = \sigma - 2 - l$):** Since
   $a_- = M(2 + l) - (2 + l) = (M - 1)(2 + l)$ and $\sigma - a_- = 2 + l$, their ratio simplifies to
   $\frac{\sigma - a_-}{a_-} = \frac{1}{M - 1}$. Because $k \le (1 + \frac{\delta}{2})\sigma U + 1 = M(M - 1)U + 1$
   and $M - 1 \ge 1$, we obtain
   $$-M U + k\,\frac{\sigma - a_-}{a_-} = -M U + \frac{k}{M - 1} \le -M U + M U + \frac{1}{M - 1} \le 1.$$
2. **Left tail ($x = +l U$, $a = a_+ = \sigma - 2 + l$):** Here $\frac{\sigma - a_+}{a_+} = -\frac{l - 2}{\sigma - 2 + l}$.
   Using $k \ge (1 + \frac{\delta}{2})\sigma U$ and $(1 + \frac{\delta}{2})(l - 2) = l + 1$, we have
   $k(l - 2) \ge (l + 1)\sigma U$. Since $\sigma = M(2 + l) > l(2 + l) > l^2$, we have
   $(l + 1)\sigma - l(\sigma - 2 + l) = \sigma + 2l - l^2 > 0$, so $k\,\frac{l - 2}{\sigma - 2 + l} \ge l U$, giving
   $$l U + k\,\frac{\sigma - a_+}{a_+} = l U - k\,\frac{l - 2}{\sigma - 2 + l} \le 0.$$

Hence the two tails sum to at most $\bigl(\frac{e}{a_-} + \frac{1}{a_+}\bigr)\sigma^{-k} =: C_1 \sigma^{-k}$.

**Step 3: Assembly (`landau_window_key`).** Fix $\sigma > 2$ and $C_1$ from Step 2, and set
$C := 2 + 2 C_0 C_1$. Given $\eta > 0$ and $U_0 \in \mathbb R$, apply `WindowHypU g θ δ` to
$\max(U_0, 1)$ to obtain $U \ge \max(U_0, 1)$ and $R$ with $\int_U^{(1+\delta)U} R(u) e^{-\theta u}\,du \le e^{\eta U}$,
and let $k \ge U$ be the integer from Step 2. Since $0 < \sigma - \theta < \sigma$ and $e^{\eta U} \ge 1$,
we have $\sigma^{-k} \le e^{\eta U} (\sigma - \theta)^{-k}$. Substituting both bounds into Step 1 gives
$$\lvert A_k(\sigma + i\gamma) \rvert + \Re A_k(\sigma)
\le \frac{2\,e^{\eta U}}{(\sigma - \theta)^k} + \frac{2 C_0 C_1}{\sigma^k}
\le \frac{(2 + 2 C_0 C_1)\,e^{\eta U}}{(\sigma - \theta)^k} \qquad \forall \gamma \in \mathbb R. \qquad \square$$

### 3.4.4 Geometric pole dichotomy and vertical perturbation

Now we examine what a pole $\rho_0 \in Z$ with $\Re\rho_0 > \theta$ forces upon the Taylor/Laurent
coefficients $A_k(z)$ from *below*.

**Lemma 3.5 (Pole bounds, dichotomy, nearest-pole perturbation, and Cauchy asymptotics).**
*Assume `PoleData g Φ Z`, $0 < \theta < \sigma$, and $2 < \sigma$.*

1. *(`landau_pole_re_le` and `landau_finite`.) Every $\rho \in Z$ satisfies $\Re\rho \le 2 < \sigma$.
   Moreover, for any $z \in \mathbb C$ and $r < \Re z$, the intersection $Z \cap \overline{B}(z, r)$
   is finite.*
2. *(`landau_dichotomy`.) Suppose there exists $\rho_0 \in Z$ with $\Re\rho_0 > \theta$. Then at
   least one of the following two alternatives holds:*
   - **(Case A: off-axis pole closer to $\Re s = \sigma$ than the pole-free disk around $\sigma$.)**
     *There exist $D \le \sigma - \theta$ and $\rho_1 \in Z$ such that $Z \cap B(\sigma, D) = \emptyset$
     and $\sigma - \Re\rho_1 < D$.*
   - **(Case B: isolated nearest real pole.)** *There exist a real number $\beta \in (\theta, \sigma)$
     with $\beta \in Z$ and a radius $r$ with $\sigma - \beta < r \le \sigma - \theta$ such that
     $Z \cap B(\sigma, r) = \{\beta\}$.*
3. *(`landau_nearest`.) In Case A, there exist $\gamma \in \mathbb R$, $\rho \in Z$, and $r \le D$
   such that $d := \lvert (\sigma + i\gamma) - \rho \rvert < r$ and
   $Z \cap B(\sigma + i\gamma, r) = \{\rho\}$.*
4. *(`landau_cauchy` and `landau_pole_asym`.) If $\Phi$ is holomorphic on $B(z, r)$, then for any
   $0 < r' < r$ there exists $M' \ge 0$ such that $\lvert A_k(z) \rvert \le M' / r'^k$ for all
   $k \in \mathbb N$. If $\rho \in B(z, r) \setminus \{z\}$, $\Phi$ is holomorphic on
   $B(z, r) \setminus \{\rho\}$, and $\Phi(s) = \frac{c}{s - \rho} + h(s)$ near $\rho$ with $h$
   analytic at $\rho$, then for any $0 < r' < r$ there exists $M \ge 0$ such that*
   $$\Bigl\lvert A_k(z) - \frac{c}{(z - \rho)^{k+1}} \Bigr\rvert \le \frac{M}{r'^k} \qquad \forall k \in \mathbb N.$$

*(Lean: `landau_pole_re_le`, `…/LandauPoleReLe.lean:29`; `landau_finite`, `…/LandauFinite.lean:9`;
`landau_dichotomy`, `…/LandauDichotomy.lean:24`; `landau_nearest`, `…/LandauNearest.lean:36`;
`landau_cauchy`, `…/LandauCauchy.lean:9`; `landau_pole_asym`, `…/LandauPoleAsym.lean:19`.)* [Lean, read]

*Proof.*
(1) If some $\rho \in Z$ had $\Re\rho > 2$, then on the closed half-plane $\Re s \ge \sigma_1 := (2 + \Re\rho)/2 > 2$
the Laplace integral bounds $\lvert \Phi(s) \rvert \le \int_0^\infty C_0 e^{-(\sigma_1 - 2)u}\,du =: M_1 < \infty$,
so $(s - \rho)\Phi(s) \to 0$ as $s \to \rho$ ($s \neq \rho$), whereas `PoleData.pole` gives
$(s - \rho)\Phi(s) = c + (s - \rho)h(s) \to c \neq 0$, a contradiction (`landau_pole_re_le`). Next,
if $r < \Re z$, the closed disk $K = \overline{B}(z, r)$ is compact and lies inside $\{\Re w \ge \Re z - r > 0\}$.
By `PoleData.iso`, every $w \in K$ has an open neighborhood $U_w$ meeting $Z$ in at most $\{w\}$;
passing to a finite subcover shows $Z \cap K$ is finite (`landau_finite`).

(2) Since $0 < \theta < \sigma$, we have $\sigma - \theta < \sigma$, so by (1) the set
$F := Z \cap \overline{B}(\sigma, \sigma - \theta)$ is finite.
- If $F = \emptyset$, then $Z \cap B(\sigma, \sigma - \theta) = \emptyset$, while $\rho_0 \in Z$
  satisfies $\sigma - \Re\rho_0 < \sigma - \theta$, giving Case A with $D = \sigma - \theta$ and $\rho_1 = \rho_0$.
- Suppose $F \neq \emptyset$, and choose $\rho_* \in F$ minimizing $d := \lvert w - \sigma \rvert$
  over $w \in F$; clearly $d \le \sigma - \theta$ and $Z \cap B(\sigma, d) = \emptyset$ (any point
  of $Z$ in $B(\sigma, d)$ would lie in $F$ and have strictly smaller distance to $\sigma$). If
  there exists $\rho_1 \in Z$ with $\sigma - \Re\rho_1 < d$, then Case A holds with $D = d$.
  Otherwise, **every** $w \in Z$ satisfies $\sigma - \Re w \ge d$. In particular:
  - for $w = \rho_0$, we get $d \le \sigma - \Re\rho_0 < \sigma - \theta$, so $d < \sigma - \theta$;
  - for any $w \in F$ achieving $\lvert w - \sigma \rvert = d$, we have
    $(\Re w - \sigma)^2 + (\Im w)^2 = d^2 \le (\sigma - \Re w)^2$ (since $\sigma - \Re w \ge d \ge 0$),
    which forces $(\Im w)^2 \le 0$, hence $\Im w = 0$ and $\Re w = \sigma - d$ (since $\Re w < \sigma$
    by (1))!
  Thus $\beta := \sigma - d$ is the **unique** element of $F$ at distance $d$ from $\sigma$
  (in particular $\rho_* = \beta \in Z$), and $\beta = \sigma - d > \sigma - (\sigma - \theta) = \theta$.
  Since $F \setminus \{\beta\}$ is finite and every $t \in F \setminus \{\beta\}$ has $\lvert t - \sigma \rvert > d$,
  setting $r_0$ to be $\min_{t \in F \setminus \{\beta\}} \lvert t - \sigma \rvert > d$ (or $d + 1$ if
  $F = \{\beta\}$) and $r := \min(r_0, \sigma - \theta)$ gives $\sigma - \beta = d < r \le \sigma - \theta$
  and $Z \cap B(\sigma, r) = \{\beta\}$, which is Case B.

(3) Assume Case A: $Z \cap B(\sigma, D) = \emptyset$ and $\rho_1 \in Z$ satisfies $\sigma - \Re\rho_1 < D$,
with $D \le \sigma - \theta < \sigma$. Choose $\varepsilon := \frac{1}{2}\min\bigl(D - (\sigma - \Re\rho_1),\,\sigma - D\bigr) > 0$,
and put $c_0 := \sigma + i\Im\rho_1$. Since $D + \varepsilon < \sigma = \Re c_0$, part (1) shows
that $F' := Z \cap \overline{B}(c_0, D + \varepsilon)$ is finite. Notice that for any two points
$a, b \in \mathbb C$ with $\Re a < \sigma$ and $\Re b < \sigma$, if $\lvert \sigma + i\gamma - a \rvert = \lvert \sigma + i\gamma - b \rvert$
for some $\gamma \in \mathbb R$, then squaring both sides gives
$$(\sigma - \Re a)^2 + (\gamma - \Im a)^2 = (\sigma - \Re b)^2 + (\gamma - \Im b)^2.$$
If $\Im a \neq \Im b$, solving this linear equation in $\gamma$ uniquely determines
$$\gamma = \frac{(\sigma - \Re a)^2 + (\Im a)^2 - (\sigma - \Re b)^2 - (\Im b)^2}{2(\Im a - \Im b)};$$
if $\Im a = \Im b$, the equation reduces to $(\Re b - \Re a)\bigl((\sigma - \Re a) + (\sigma - \Re b)\bigr) = 0$,
and since $(\sigma - \Re a) + (\sigma - \Re b) > 0$, we get $\Re a = \Re b$, so $a = b$ (`landau_nearest_equi`).
Because $F' \times F'$ is finite, the set $B \subset \mathbb R$ of these finitely many tie-heights
is finite, whereas the open interval $(\Im\rho_1 - \varepsilon, \Im\rho_1 + \varepsilon)$ is infinite!
Pick $\gamma \in (\Im\rho_1 - \varepsilon, \Im\rho_1 + \varepsilon) \setminus B$, and put
$c := \sigma + i\gamma$. Then $\lvert c - c_0 \rvert = \lvert \gamma - \Im\rho_1 \rvert < \varepsilon$,
so $\rho_1 \in F'$ and
$$\lvert c - \rho_1 \rvert \le \lvert c - c_0 \rvert + \lvert c_0 - \rho_1 \rvert
< \varepsilon + (\sigma - \Re\rho_1) < D.$$
Let $\rho \in F'$ minimize $\lvert c - w \rvert$ over $w \in F'$, and set $d := \lvert c - \rho \rvert \le \lvert c - \rho_1 \rvert < D$.
Because $\gamma \notin B$, no $w \in F' \setminus \{\rho\}$ can have $\lvert c - w \rvert = d$, so
every $w \in F' \setminus \{\rho\}$ satisfies $\lvert c - w \rvert > d$. Defining
$r_0 := \min_{w \in F' \setminus \{\rho\}} \lvert c - w \rvert > d$ (or $d + 1$ if $F' = \{\rho\}$)
and $r := \min(r_0, D)$ gives $d < r \le D$ and $Z \cap B(c, r) = \{\rho\}$ (since any $w \in Z \cap B(c, r)$
has $\lvert w - c_0 \rvert \le \lvert w - c \rvert + \lvert c - c_0 \rvert < D + \varepsilon$, hence
$w \in F'$, hence $w = \rho$).

(4) If $G$ is holomorphic on $B(z, r)$ and $0 < r' < r$, Cauchy's integral formula on the circle
$\lvert w - z \rvert = r'$ bounds the $k$-th Taylor coefficient by
$$\Bigl\lvert \frac{G^{(k)}(z)}{k!} \Bigr\rvert
\le \frac{1}{2\pi r'^k}\int_0^{2\pi} \lvert G(z + r' e^{i\vartheta}) \rvert\,d\vartheta =: \frac{M'}{r'^k}$$
(`landau_cauchy`). If $\Phi$ has a single simple pole at $\rho \in B(z, r) \setminus \{z\}$ with
principal part $\frac{c}{s - \rho}$, then $G(s) := \Phi(s) - \frac{c}{s - \rho}$ (extended by $h(\rho)$
at $s = \rho$) is holomorphic on the entire disk $B(z, r)$. Differentiating
$\Phi(s) = G(s) + c(s - \rho)^{-1}$ $k$ times at $z \neq \rho$ and using
$\frac{d^k}{ds^k}(s - \rho)^{-1}\big|_{s=z} = (-1)^k k! (z - \rho)^{-(k+1)}$ gives
$$A_k(z) - \frac{c}{(z - \rho)^{k+1}} = \frac{(-1)^k G^{(k)}(z)}{k!},$$
whose modulus is bounded by $M / r'^k$ via Cauchy's inequality applied to $G$ (`landau_pole_asym`). $\square$

### 3.4.5 The Landau-window criterion (`landau_window`, `lemmaL`, `zeroFree_of_rpow_window`)

Putting Lemma 3.4 and Lemma 3.5 together produces an immediate exponential-growth clash whenever a
pole tries to cross $\Re s = \theta$.

**Theorem 3.6 (The Landau-window criterion).**
1. *(Abstract criterion, `landau_window`.) Assume `PoleData g Φ Z`, $0 < \theta < 1$, $\delta > 0$,
   and `WindowHypU g θ δ`. Then every $\rho \in Z$ satisfies $\Re\rho \le \theta$.*
2. *(Character-family criteria, `lemmaL` and `zeroFree_of_rpow_window`.) Let $F : \mathrm{CharFamily}$,
   $0 < \theta < 1$, and $\delta > 0$. If `WindowHyp F θ δ` holds — in particular, if there exists
   $C \in \mathbb R$ such that for every $Y_0$ there is $Y \ge Y_0$ with $G_m(F, y) \le C y^\theta$
   for all $y \in [Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$ — then `ZeroFree F θ` holds: for every $i \in \iota$ and every
   $s \in \mathbb C$ with $\Re s > \theta$, we have $L(s, \chi_i) \neq 0$.*

*(Lean: `landau_growth_contra`, `…/LandauGrowthContra.lean:9`; `landau_window_contra` and
`landau_window`, `…/landauWindow.lean:20,83`; `lemmaL`, `…/lemmaL.lean:13`;
`zeroFree_of_rpow_window`, `…/ZeroFreeOfRpowWindow.lean:11`.)* [Lean, read]

*Proof.*
(1) First note a one-line growth contradiction (`landau_growth_contra`, `landau_window_contra`): if
real constants satisfy $c_0 > 0$ and $0 < d < e \le E$, then it is **impossible** that for every
$\eta > 0$ and $U_0 \in \mathbb R$ there exist $U \ge U_0$ and an integer $k \ge U$ with
$$\frac{c_0}{d^k} \le \frac{M_0}{e^k} + \frac{C\,e^{\eta U}}{E^k}.$$
Indeed, since $e \le E$ and $e^{\eta U} \ge 1$ for $U \ge 0$, the right-hand side is at most
$(\lvert M_0 \rvert + \lvert C \rvert) e^{\eta U} / e^k$. Setting $L := \log(e / d) > 0$ and
choosing $\eta := L / 2 > 0$, we have $e^k = d^k e^{k L}$ while $e^{\eta U} \le e^{k L / 2}$ for
$k \ge U \ge 0$; thus as soon as $U \ge \frac{2(\lvert M_0 \rvert + \lvert C \rvert)}{c_0 L}$ (so
$c_0 e^{L U / 2} \ge c_0 (1 + L U / 2) > \lvert M_0 \rvert + \lvert C \rvert$), we get
$$(\lvert M_0 \rvert + \lvert C \rvert)\frac{e^{L U / 2}}{e^k}
< c_0\,\frac{e^{L U}}{e^k} \le c_0\,\frac{e^{k L}}{d^k e^{k L}} = \frac{c_0}{d^k},$$
a contradiction!

Now suppose for a contradiction that there exists $\rho_0 \in Z$ with $\Re\rho_0 > \theta$. Let
$\sigma > 2$ and $C > 0$ be given by Lemma 3.4 (`landau_window_key`), and apply Lemma 3.5(2)
(`landau_dichotomy`), noting $\Re\rho < \sigma$ for all $\rho \in Z$ by Lemma 3.5(1).

- **In Case A:** $Z \cap B(\sigma, D) = \emptyset$ with $D \le \sigma - \theta$, and Lemma 3.5(3)
  (`landau_nearest`) supplies $\gamma \in \mathbb R$, $\rho \in Z$, and $r \le D$ such that
  $d := \lvert z - \rho \rvert < r$ (where $z = \sigma + i\gamma$) and $Z \cap B(z, r) = \{\rho\}$.
  Since $r \le D \le \sigma - \theta < \sigma = \Re z = \Re\sigma$, both disks $B(z, r)$ and
  $B(\sigma, D)$ lie entirely inside $\{\Re w > 0\}$ (`landau_window_re_pos`), so by `PoleData.diff`
  the function $\Phi$ is holomorphic on $B(z, r) \setminus \{\rho\}$ and on all of $B(\sigma, D)$.
  Let $c \neq 0$ be the residue of $\Phi$ at $\rho$, and set $e := (d + r)/2$, so
  $0 < d < e < r \le D \le \sigma - \theta =: E$. Applying Lemma 3.5(4) at radius $r' = e$ both to
  $B(z, r) \setminus \{\rho\}$ (`landau_pole_asym`) and to the pole-free disk $B(\sigma, D)$
  (`landau_cauchy`) gives constants $M, M' \ge 0$ such that for all $k \in \mathbb N$,
  $$\lvert A_k(z) \rvert \ge \Bigl\lvert \frac{c}{(z - \rho)^{k+1}} \Bigr\rvert - \frac{M}{e^k}
  = \frac{\lvert c \rvert / d}{d^k} - \frac{M}{e^k}
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \Re A_k(\sigma) \ge -\lvert A_k(\sigma) \rvert \ge -\frac{M'}{e^k}.$$
  Adding these and comparing with Lemma 3.4 at $\gamma$ gives, for every $\eta > 0$ and $U_0$, some
  $U \ge U_0$ and $k \ge U$ with
  $$\frac{\lvert c \rvert / d}{d^k} \le \frac{M + M'}{e^k} + \frac{C\,e^{\eta U}}{(\sigma - \theta)^k},$$
  contradicting the growth lemma above (`c₀ = ‖c‖ / d > 0`)!

- **In Case B:** There exist a real pole $\beta \in Z \cap (\theta, \sigma)$ and $r$ with
  $0 < d := \sigma - \beta < r \le \sigma - \theta =: E$ such that $Z \cap B(\sigma, r) = \{\beta\}$.
  Again $B(\sigma, r) \subset \{\Re w > 0\}$, so $\Phi$ is holomorphic on $B(\sigma, r) \setminus \{\beta\}$.
  By `PoleData.pole` at the real point $\beta$ ($\Im\beta = 0$), the residue $c$ of $\Phi$ at
  $\beta$ satisfies $\Im c = 0$ and $\Re c > 0$. Setting $e := (d + r)/2 \in (d, r)$ and applying
  Lemma 3.5(4) at $z = \sigma$ ($\gamma = 0$) gives $M \ge 0$ such that for all $k \in \mathbb N$,
  $$\Re A_k(\sigma) \ge \Re\Bigl(\frac{c}{(\sigma - \beta)^{k+1}}\Bigr) - \frac{M}{e^k}
  = \frac{\Re c / d}{d^k} - \frac{M}{e^k}.$$
  Since $\lvert A_k(\sigma) \rvert \ge 0$, comparing $\lvert A_k(\sigma) \rvert + \Re A_k(\sigma)$
  with Lemma 3.4 at $\gamma = 0$ gives
  $$\frac{\Re c / d}{d^k} \le \frac{M}{e^k} + \frac{C\,e^{\eta U}}{(\sigma - \theta)^k},$$
  again contradicting the growth lemma (`c₀ = c.re / d > 0`)!

Thus no $\rho_0 \in Z$ can satisfy $\Re\rho_0 > \theta$, proving `landau_window`.

(2) Given $F : \mathrm{CharFamily}$ and `WindowHyp F θ δ`, Theorem 3.2 supplies $\Phi$ with
`PoleData (gm F) Φ (zeroSet F)`, and Lemma 3.3 converts `WindowHyp F θ δ` to `WindowHypU (gm F) θ δ`.
By part (1), every $\rho \in Z(F)$ satisfies $\Re\rho \le \theta$. Now let $i \in \iota$ and let
$s \in \mathbb C$ satisfy $\Re s > \theta$ and $L(s, \chi_i) = 0$. If $\Re s \ge 1$, this
contradicts classical non-vanishing on $\Re s \ge 1$ (`charFamily_LFunction_ne_zero_of_one_le_re`);
if $\Re s < 1$, then $\Re s > \theta > 0$ puts $s \in Z(F)$, whence $\Re s \le \theta$, contradicting
$\Re s > \theta$. This proves `lemmaL`, and combining `lemmaL` with Lemma 3.3 immediately gives
`zeroFree_of_rpow_window`. $\square$

\Lambda.\mathrm{wt}(\psi) & \text{if }\psi \neq \mathrm{PChar.one}.

## 3.5 Remarks

### 3.5.1 Direct limit versus zero-free-region bootstrapping

In classical analytic number theory, zero-free regions near $\Re s = 1$ are sometimes widened
iteratively, feeding an already established zero-free strip for $L(s,\psi)$ back into an explicit
formula for $L(s,\psi)$ to obtain a slightly wider strip. A reader checking for circularity should
note that **nothing of the sort happens in `level_of_tiedWin`** [Lean, read]:

- For each fixed target point $s$ with $\Re s > 1/2$, Theorem 3.7(2) picks a *single* small
  $\varepsilon > 0$ so that $\theta = 1/2 + \frac{1}{2}\min(\Re s - 1/2, 1/2)$ lies strictly
  between $1/2 + (e_D + e_L + 7)\varepsilon$ and $\Re s$, and invokes `FormBAt` once.
- Inside the proof of `FormB` (Proposition 3.1 through Theorem 3.6), the **only** zero-free input
  ever used about any character in the level is Mathlib's classical non-vanishing on the closed
  half-plane $\Re s \ge 1$ (`charFamily_LFunction_ne_zero_of_one_le_re`, used solely to verify
  $\rho \neq 1$ and $\beta < 1$ in Theorem 3.2 and `lemmaL`).
- As Chapters 4 and 5 will verify, the auxiliary zero-free boxes of width $1/400$ in `EAtH` /
  `HostConditions` apply exclusively to **quadratic twists** of conductor divisible by the huge
  auxiliary primes $q$ or $d$ (`Bounded` characters, disjoint from `KEPT`), never to the target
  cyclic level `Level.cyc ψ` itself [Lean, read]
  (`src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/RamificationFrobenius/TotientGaloisClasses/Analytic.lean:136,154`).

### 3.5.2 Why $(3o + 1)^{-1}$ appears in `Level.wt`, and unused cousins in the archive

Two small features of the code are worth explaining so they do not puzzle a reader opening the
files [Expository]:

1. **The weight $(3o + 1)^{-1}$.** In `landau_window` and `poleData`, *any* strictly positive
   weights $a_i > 0$ work equally well. Why does `Level.wt` (`ExplicitFormulaErrorOfHostConditions.lean:36`)
   choose the specific number $(3\Lambda.o + 1)^{-1}$? Look seventy lines lower in the same file at
   `Frame.lam` (`:135`):
   $$\varphi.\mathrm{lam}(\gamma) = \frac{\text{if }\gamma \in A_0 \text{ then } 4 \text{ else } 3}{3o + 1}.$$
   When a finite group $G / A_0$ of order $o$ has $1$ identity coset of weight $4/(3o+1)$ and
   $o - 1$ non-identity cosets of weight $3/(3o+1)$, the total mass is
   $\frac{4 + 3(o - 1)}{3o + 1} = \frac{3o + 1}{3o + 1} = 1$, while the Fourier transform of that
   coset distribution equals $1$ at the trivial character and $\frac{4 - 3}{3o + 1} = \frac{1}{3o + 1} > 0$
   at every nontrivial character of $G / A_0$! That finite-Fourier identity is the subject of
   Chapter 5 (`core_gm`); Chapter 3 needs from it only that $(3o + 1)^{-1} > 0$ [Lean, read].
2. **Unused parallel definitions.** Three files we read contain alternative propositions that the
   main proof path does not invoke [Lean, read]:
   - `Formalism/StatementOC.lean:23–71` defines order-comparison steps (`OCStep`, `OCAvg`,
     `RiemannHypothesisOfLowerBounds`) comparing `Gz` with real quadratic characters; the live
     spine uses **only** `Formalism.Sz` and `Formalism.Gz` (`:11,13`) from that file.
   - `DedekindLFunctions/LandauZeroFreeCriterion.lean:33–62` defines `famD`, `GmLevel`, and
     `LandauZeroFreeCriterion` for even characters modulo $2^{j+2}$ under an eventual bound
     $\forall y \ge y_0$; the live spine uses `PoleData` and `WindowHypU` (`:15,25`) instead.
   - `TotientGaloisClasses/TopWin.lean:34,101–134` defines `TiedAll` and `level_of_tiedAll` for
     eventual bounds holding for *all* $x \ge x_0$, alongside the windowed theorems `level_of_tiedWin`
     and `zeroFreeOn_of_tiedWin_cyc` (`:68,90`) that `zeroFreeAll_of_topG` actually calls.

### 3.5.4 Masullo's counterpart

In Masullo's *The Music of the Primes*, the analytic bridge between prime powers and zeros of
$L$-functions sits in **Appendix B.3 ("Asymptotic Residue Theorem", pp. 604–605)**, **Appendix B.4
("Explicit Formulas for Hecke L-functions", pp. 606–625)**, and the planned **Section 13.1
("Recollections on the Explicit Formulas", p. 522)**, feeding the trace formula of Chapters 10–12
[Paper; Masullo pp. 522, 604–625].

Comparing the two bridges highlights how fundamentally the two architectures diverge right after
the functional equation [Expository]:

| Feature | Lean archive (Alpöge with Claude) | Masullo's draft |
|---|---|---|
| Analytic transform | Riesz-smoothed prime sum $G_\chi(y)$ and Laplace transform $\Phi(s) = \int_0^\infty G_m(e^u) e^{-su}\,du$ [Lean, read] | Weil-style explicit formula pairing smooth compactly supported test functions $\varphi \in \mathcal D(C_F)$ against zeros (Theorem B.4.4.7, p. 623, proved on pp. 623–625) [Paper; Masullo pp. 607, 623–625] |
| Equality vs. inequality | **One-sided inequality** $G_m(y) \le y^\theta$ on sparse windows $[Y, Y^{3/2}]$ [Lean, read] | **Exact identity**: spectral trace = geometric orbital trace (Theorem 12.5.1.1, p. 519) [Paper; Masullo p. 519] |

| Where $1/2$ enters | In the upper bound exponent $1/2 + (e_D + e_L + 7)\varepsilon$ supplied by `core_gm` (Chapter 5) [Lean, read] | Built into the normalization $\lvert g \rvert_v^{1/2}$ of local principal-value distributions (eq. (B.4.4.20), p. 625; §1.2.1, p. 13) [Paper; Masullo pp. 13, 625] |

**Summary of the chapter.** For any admissible family of Dirichlet characters with positive weights,
the Laplace transform $\Phi(s)$ of $G_m(e^u)$ is meromorphic on $\Re s > 0$ with simple poles at
the zeros and positive real residues at real zeros in $(0, 1)$ (Theorem 3.2) [Lean, read]. Concentrating
the Poisson kernel $u^k e^{-\sigma u}/k!$ inside a window $[U, (1+\delta)U]$ and perturbing the
evaluation height $\gamma$ off finitely many perpendicular bisectors proves that a one-sided bound
$G_m(y) \le C y^\theta$ on windows $[Y, Y^{1+\delta}]$ rules out all zeros on $\Re s > \theta$
(Theorem 3.6) [Lean, read]. Packaging the cyclic group generated by a primitive character $\psi$
into `Level.cyc ψ` and picking $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough for each target $s$ reduces
`ZeroFreeAll` to proving the windowed bound $\Lambda.\mathrm{Gm}(x) \le x^{1/2 + (e_D + e_L + 7)\varepsilon}$
at scale $\lambda = 3/2$ (Theorem 3.7) [Lean, read]. Chapters 4 and 5 show how the host fields and
`core_gm` produce that bound.
