# 8.4.12.1 Why restriction to one place preserves the same InClass witness

**Motivation [Expository].** The place operator in [§8.4.12](08-divisibility-at-inert-primes.md#8412-transporting-the-restricted-kernel-jet-to-the-three-frames) sees coefficients in the variables of one place. The generic Gauss kernels also need a class expansion of the entire rescaled series in those variables. Its integral factors form a single family $(g _ i) _ {i\ge0}$ that works at every degree. Restriction sends each member of this family to its restriction at the chosen place. The proof has to account for both multiplication and the degree cutoff in the class formula.

**Example 1 (extracting and relabelling variables).** Retain the ambient variables $z,x$ of a series in $x,y,z$, labelling them $t,q$ in that order. The exponent $(a,b)$ in $t,q$ is sent to $(b,0,a)$ in $x,y,z$. For $A=2+z+y$ and $B=1+3x+4z$, the surviving product is $(2+t)(1+3q+4t)=2+9t+6q+4t^2+3tq$. For instance, $[tq]\operatorname{res}(AB)=\left[xz\right]{(AB)}=3$. The coefficient at $tq$ is unchanged and its total degree is still two. If each variable is scaled by $2$, this coefficient becomes $2^2\cdot3=12$ in either order. The other-place terms from $yB$ disappear.

**Example 2 (a cyclic place model).** Work over $\mathbb Q _ 5$, take a place with six slots and use one row and one column. Include one other-place variable $w$. Write $Z _ s$ for the six original variables and $z _ s$ for the relabelled variables, with $e(s)=s\bmod 6$. In slot $0$, $\operatorname{posSub}(0,2l)$ runs through $0,4,2,0,\ldots$ at $l=0,1,2,3,\ldots$. The first four nonzero terms of the local sparse series are $z _ 0-z _ 4^{25}+z _ 2^{625}-z _ 0^{15625}$. Slot $1$ starts with $z _ 1-z _ 5^{25}$. Each exponent is $5^{2l}$, so a return to the same slot still gives a new degree.

Put $u _ 0=1$ and $u _ s=0$ for $s\ne0$. In the original variables let $H$ be the fifth power of the slot-$0$ sparse series times the slot-$1$ sparse series. In the restricted variables let $H'$ be the corresponding product. Its degree-six term is $z _ 0^5z _ 1$. In degree $30$, replacing one of the five linear factors in the fifth power gives $-5z _ 0^4z _ 4^{25}z _ 1$. Replacing the slot-$1$ factor gives $-z _ 0^5z _ 5^{25}$.

Take $g _ 0=g _ 1=1+w$, $g _ 2=1$, and $g _ i=0$ for $i>2$. Put $A=g _ 0+Hg _ 1/5+H^2/25$ and define $F$ by dividing every variable of $A$ by $2$, including $w$. With $\pi=2$ the rescaled input is $F(2Z)=A$, where $Z$ here includes all seven variables. These $g _ i$ have integral coefficients and satisfy the class formula at every index. The order of $H$ is six, so terms omitted by the degree cutoff already have zero coefficient. After restriction and scaling the result is $1+H'/5+(H')^2/25$, with witness $1,1,1,0,\ldots$. Its coefficients include $1/5$ at $z _ 0^5z _ 1$, $1/25$ at $z _ 0^{10}z _ 1^2$, and $-1$ and $-1/5$ at the two degree-$30$ monomials above. The square starts in degree $12$ and each replacement from a sparse factor adds at least $24$, so it contributes nothing in degree $30$. This model uses the original place formulas and the arbitrary scalar allowed by the class transport theorem.

**Definitions [Lean, read].** For a variable set $J$, an exponent $n:J\to\mathbb N$ has finite support and total degree $|n|=\sum _ {x\in J}n(x)$. Write $[X^n]A$ for its coefficient. For $f:L\to J$, `Finsupp.mapDomain` pushes $n$ forward to $f _ \ast n=\sum _ {x\in\operatorname{supp}(n)}n(x)\delta _ {f(x)}$, where $\delta _ y$ is the unit exponent at $y$. Define $R _ f=\operatorname{resV}(f,-)$ by $[X^n]R _ f A=[X^{f _ \ast n}]A$. The scalar rescaling $\operatorname{atPi}(\pi,A)$ multiplies the coefficient at $n$ by $\pi^{|n|}$.

Fix a prime $p$, a system $X:\mathrm{Sys}(p)$, a place $v$, and $j\in\mathbb N$. Put $N=n'(v)$, $\nu=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$, $r _ 0=NN(X.G.P.m,X.t,j)$ and $r _ 1=NN(X.G.P.m,X.t,j+1)$. The system gives $N=2\nu>0$. Let $J=X.\mathrm{ZA}(j)$, the disjoint union of the slot and matrix-index sets of the places. The local set is $L=\mathbb Z/(2\nu)\mathbb Z\times\mathrm{Fin}(r _ 0)\times\mathrm{Fin}(r _ 1)$. The bijection $e$ (the map `ea_e X v`) sends a slot to its residue class. The variable map $\epsilon$ (the map `ea_eps X v j`) sends $(s,a,b)$ to $(v,e^{-1}(s),a,b)$.

Let $K'$ be an ultrametric normed field, $B=\lbrace c\in K':\lVert c\rVert\le1\rbrace $ and $P=(p:K')$. For a control $H$ and a series $A$, the source `ea_InClassA` over $B$ asks for a single family $g:\mathbb N\to K'[[J]]$ with $\lVert[X^\alpha]g _ i\rVert\le1$ for every $i,\alpha$, and
$[X^\alpha]A=\sum _ {i=0}^{|\alpha|}(P^i)^{-1}[X^\alpha](H^i g _ i)$ for every $\alpha$.
The target `G7.InClass` uses this same coefficient formula on $L$, with integrality expressed directly by the norm inequality. The inverse uses the Lean convention $0^{-1}=0$.

**Lemma (restriction and rescaling) [Lean, read].** If $f:L\to J$ is injective, $R _ f$ preserves constants, sums, products and powers. For every $f$, the degree of $f _ \ast n$ is $|n|$, and $R _ f\operatorname{atPi}(\pi,A)=\operatorname{atPi}(\pi,R _ f A)$. On either variable set $\operatorname{atPi}(\pi,A)$ is the formal substitution $X _ x\mapsto\pi X _ x$.

**Proof.** The coefficient formula is the definition of $R _ f$ (`ea_coeff_resV`). Summing the coordinates of $f _ \ast n$ counts each original exponent once, even when several exponents land at the same variable (`Finsupp.degree_mapDomain`). Under injectivity the coefficient of $f _ \ast n$ at $f(x)$ is $n(x)$, so $f _ \ast$ is injective and its inverse on exponents supported in $f(L)$ is `comapDomain`. The rule for constants follows from $f _ \ast n=0$ exactly when $n=0$.

For products write the coefficient at $f _ \ast n$ as a sum over the finite set of pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$ with $\alpha+\beta=f _ \ast n$. Both exponents are bounded coordinatewise by $f _ \ast n$ because they have nonnegative entries. Thus both are supported in $f(L)$. Pull them back along $f$. Additivity and the inverse identities give a pair with sum $n$. Conversely every pair $(m,k)$ with $m+k=n$ pushes to such a pair, and the two constructions are inverse. The two coefficient sums have equal summands under this bijection. This is the bijection in `ea_resV_mul`. Addition and zero follow coefficientwise, and constants provide the unit. Hence the ring homomorphism `ea_resHom` transports powers as well.

The coefficient in either order of restriction and rescaling is $\pi^{|n|}[X^{f _ \ast n}]A$. In the scalar substitution each variable image has zero constant term. The monomial at $n$ acquires the factor $\prod _ x\pi^{n(x)}=\pi^{|n|}$, as in `MvPowerSeries.coeff_rescale` and `rescale_eq_subst` used by `ea_atPi_subst`. At the zero index the scaling factor is $1$.

**Lemma (transport of the controls) [Lean, read].** The map $\epsilon$ is injective. With $u' _ s=u _ {e^{-1}(s)}$, restriction gives
$R _ \epsilon(X.\mathrm{sSA}(K',j,v,t,a,b))=\mathrm{G7.sPos}(p,e(t),(a,b))$ and
$R _ \epsilon(X.\mathrm{hhA}(K',j,v,u))=\mathrm{G7.hhS}(p,u')$.

**Proof.** Equality after applying $\epsilon$ yields equality of all three entries in the fibre over $v$. Applying $e$ to the first entry recovers the original residue class. This is `ea_eps_inj`.

The slot $\operatorname{posSub}(t,d)$ is the representative of $(t+Nd-d)\bmod N$. Since $N>0$, $d\le t+Nd$ and the natural subtraction is exact. Passing to residues gives $e(\operatorname{posSub}(t,d))=e(t)-d$. Likewise $e(\operatorname{posAdd}(t,1))=e(t)+1$. For a local exponent $n$, both sparse coefficient sums use $0\le l\le |n|$ by degree conservation. In the original sum the term $(-1)^l$ is selected exactly when $\epsilon _ \ast n$ is the singleton of weight $p^{2l}$ at $(v,\operatorname{posSub}(t,2l),a,b)$. That singleton is the pushforward of the singleton of weight $(p^2)^l$ at $(e(t)-2l,a,b)$. Injectivity of the exponent map makes the two tests equivalent. This proves the identity at every coefficient (`ea_res_sSA`).

The original control sums $u _ t$ times $\mathrm{sSA} _ {t,a,b}^p\mathrm{sSA} _ {\operatorname{posAdd}(t,1),a,b}$ over $t,a,b$. The ring homomorphism transports each constant, power, product and sum. Reindex $t$ by $s=e(t)$ and combine the inner sums into the sum over pairs $(a,b)$. The next slot is $s+1$ and the multiplier is $u' _ s$. This gives the cyclic control (`ea_res_hhA`).

**Theorem (class transport for the original place, `ea_brClass`) [Lean, read].** For every place $v$, stage $j$, ultrametric normed field $K'$, scalar $\pi\in K'$, parameter $u:\mathrm{Fin}(n'(v))\to K'$ and series $F\in K'[[X.\mathrm{ZA}(j)]]$, suppose $\operatorname{atPi}(\pi,F)$ belongs to `ea_InClassA` for the unit ball and control $H=X.\mathrm{hhA}(K',j,v,u)$. Then $\operatorname{atPi}(\pi,R _ \epsilon F)$ belongs to `G7.InClass` for $P$ and $\mathrm{G7.hhS}(p,u')$. For each input witness $g$ the output witness is the entire family $i\mapsto R _ \epsilon g _ i$.

**Proof.** Fix an input family $g$ before taking any target exponent. Put $A=\operatorname{atPi}(\pi,F)$, $H'=R _ \epsilon H$ and $g' _ i=R _ \epsilon g _ i$. For all $i,n$ we have $\lVert[X^n]g' _ i\rVert=\lVert[X^{\epsilon _ \ast n}]g _ i\rVert\le1$. For any $n$ the input equality at $\epsilon _ \ast n$ gives
$[X^n]R _ \epsilon A=\sum _ {i=0}^{|n|}(P^i)^{-1}[X^{\epsilon _ \ast n}](H^i g _ i)
=\sum _ {i=0}^{|n|}(P^i)^{-1}[X^n]((H')^i g' _ i)$.
The first equality uses the original cutoff transported by degree conservation. The second uses the product and power identities inside each summand. Since $n$ was arbitrary after fixing the family, the two clauses of `InClass` hold with this one witness. The two lemmas identify $R _ \epsilon A$ with the required substitution and $H'$ with the cyclic control. These are the rewrites and the two witness obligations of `EaDegreeMapDomain.lean:162–172`.

**Remark (what injectivity buys).** For the same coefficient-extraction rule with a collision, take $f:\lbrace x,y\rbrace \to\lbrace z\rbrace $ sending both variables to $z$. Then $R _ f z=x+y$, but $R _ f(z^2)=x^2+xy+y^2$ while $(R _ f z)^2=x^2+2xy+y^2$. Total degree is conserved. At $xy$, the two splittings into unit exponents both push to the same splitting at $z^2$. This shows why injectivity is necessary for multiplicativity of this extraction on all series over a nonzero coefficient ring. The same degree-two test applies to any pair of colliding variables.

Class membership itself can fail under the collision. Over $\mathbb Q _ 5$ take $H=z$, $A=z^2/25$ and the sole nonzero witness $g _ 2=1$. If the extracted series belonged to the class for $H'=x+y$, then in degree two multiplication by $25$ and reduction modulo $5$ would give $x^2+xy+y^2=c(x+y)^2$, where $c$ is the reduction of the constant coefficient of the proposed second witness. The $i=0,1$ terms disappear by integrality, and $i=2$ contributes only that constant in this degree. The coefficient at $x^2$ forces $c=1$ while that at $xy$ forces $2c=1$ in $\mathbb F _ 5$. For a specific input, even a colliding map can transport a witness, for example when $H=0$ and only $g _ 0$ is nonzero. The actual place inclusion is injective. Forward identification of several variables uses a sum of coefficients at colliding monomials, as in Mathlib's `rename`. **Remark (where the theorem is used).** Norm normalization, the root equation for $\pi$, and the unit bounds on $u$ enter at the Gauss-kernel calls. In `FirstTheoremPlaceBound.lean:20–30` the three calls use the same packet $D,X,Y$, the same $v,j$, and $j<X.\mathrm{mm}$. Each passes `ea_restSU X Y hG`, `nm_restNorm_of_place Y v`, the jet input and `ea_brClass X`. In each `ea_lowN` callback the norm extension and integral input produce a fixed $u$ and a class premise for the rescaled $F$. After the jet test, the branch with a local coefficient identity passes the class of $(R _ \epsilon F)(\pi X)$ to `kernel_a_one_generic`, `kernel_a_two_generic` or `kernel_b_generic`. The frame jet uses $R _ \epsilon F$ before scaling. In the third call `ea_brScale` first extracts the Hecke multiplier for $1\le i\le r$. Each call may use its own norm-extension witness. The upstream `ea_RestSU`, the generic kernel estimates including `kernel_b_generic`, `minor_hits`, and the geometry in `admitted D X Y` enter at their recorded scopes.

**Remark (a witness on the original variables).** For any fixed inputs of the theorem, take $F=1$, $g _ 0=1$ and $g _ i=0$ for $i>0$. The input equality holds since the $i=0$ term is the coefficient of $1$, which rescaling preserves. Restriction carries this same family of constants to the local variable set. The cyclic example also uses nonzero higher members of the family.
